# Slides 05.19: Careers in Textiles, Apparel, and Design, and Unit 5 Review and Test

Lesson 5.19, two days (days 142 and 143). Slides 1 to 14 are Day 1. Slides 15 to 20 are Day 2.

* * *

## Slide 1: Day 1. Do now

- List every job you can think of that has something to do with clothing.
- Sixty seconds. List form.

Notes: take the list on the board fast. Most classes produce designer, model, and store worker, and stop. Hook: point at the display tables. There are thirteen jobs on my list. Three of them are things you did this month: you repaired a garment, you inspected four garments for quality, and you designed and built a product from a used material.

## Slide 2: Day 1. Careers, 1 to 3: the hands

- **Tailor.** Makes and alters garments to fit one specific body. Measures, marks, cuts, stitches. Often self-employed.
- **Alterations specialist.** Hems, takes in seams, replaces zippers and buttons, all day. In a dry cleaner, a bridal shop, a department store, or a shop of their own.
- **Upholsterer.** Strips a chair to the frame and rebuilds it with new springs, foam, and fabric. Furniture, car interiors, boat seats.
- All three: **train on the job or through an apprenticeship.** Two of them are commonly small businesses.

Image: a tape measure and chalk on a jacket shoulder; a rack of pinned hems with paper tickets; a chair stripped to its frame with a roll of fabric beside it.

## Slide 3: Day 1. Careers, 4 to 6: the care and the machines

- **Dry cleaner and garment care specialist.** Cleans, presses, spot-treats, and repairs. Reads every care symbol and decides how a garment can be cleaned without ruining it.
- **Sewing machine technician.** Services and repairs home and industrial machines: timing, tension, feed, motors. A stopped machine costs money every minute.
- **Quality inspector.** Checks finished garments against a written spec: measurements, seam type, stitches per inch, thread ends, labels, closures. Rejects what fails.
- **You already did the quality inspector's job in Lesson 5.13.**

## Slide 4: Day 1. Careers, 7 to 9: the design side

- **Fashion designer.** Decides what a garment will be. Sketches it, picks the fabric, chooses the details. Four-year degree plus a portfolio is the common route.
- **Technical designer.** Turns the sketch into the exact measurements, seam types, and instructions a factory can build from. Fits the sample and writes down every correction.
- **Costume designer.** Builds clothing for a play, a film, or a dance company, to a character, a period, a budget, and a deadline that does not move.

## Slide 5: Day 1. Careers, 10 to 12: the numbers side

- **Merchandiser.** Decides what the store shows, where it sits, and at what price.
- **Buyer.** Decides what the store buys, how much, and when, months before customers see it. Guesses what people will want and lives with being wrong.
- **Sustainability analyst.** Measures a company's material, water, energy, and waste impact and writes the report that says what it actually did. **This job did not exist thirty years ago.** Where did it come from? (Lesson 5.14.)

## Slide 6: Day 1. Career 13: the lab

- **Textile scientist.** Tests fiber and fabric for strength, shrinkage, colorfastness, water resistance, and flammability. Develops new fabrics and finishes.
- **Writes the standard that the quality inspector checks against.**
- Four-year degree, usually with chemistry or materials science. Some jobs want a graduate degree.

Image: a lab bench with a machine pulling a fabric strip until it tears, and a rack of dyed swatches.

## Slide 7: Day 1. The pathway sort

- **Train on the job or apprenticeship**
- **Certificate or two-year degree**
- **Four-year degree**
- Sort all thirteen cards at your station.
- Then mark: the **two** commonly run as a small business, and the **one** that most needs a science background.
- Some belong in more than one column. Pick the most common and be ready to argue.

## Slide 8: Day 1. What "apprenticeship" means

- You learn the job **while being paid** by somebody who already does it.
- No tuition. You start earning.
- It takes longer to get good, and it depends on finding the person.
- Tailor, alterations, upholstery, dry cleaning, machine repair: all commonly learned this way.
- Fashion designer is the job everybody names and the one with the longest, least certain route.
- Alterations is the job nobody names and the one with the steadiest local work.

Notes: do not editorialize. Let the sort say it, then ask the deepen-learning question about steady work.

## Slide 9: Day 1. Would I like this? Answer honestly.

1. Do I like working with my hands for hours?
2. Do I like fixing things other people gave up on?
3. Do I want to be around people all day, rather than mostly alone?
4. Do I like measuring and being exact?
5. Do I like deciding what things should look like?
6. Do I like numbers and money more than fabric?
7. Do I want to run my own thing rather than work for somebody?
8. Am I willing to keep going when it does not work the first time?

- Yes, no, or maybe. **Nobody sees this but you, and it is not a grade.**

## Slide 11: Day 1. The FACS-to-work bridge: the five-repair portfolio

- Five photographs. Five lines. One folder.
- Each line says **what was wrong** and **what you did**.
- Entry one is the item on the table behind you. You already wrote the description: it is your display card.
- Four more: a hem, a seam, a patch, a button. You can already do all four.

## Slide 12: Day 1. Who looks at that folder

- An alterations shop, a dry cleaner, a costume shop, or a tailor, when a sixteen-year-old asks for work.
- A high school CTE apparel or design program, when you apply.

## Slide 13: Day 1. The ladder from here

1. Practice at home. Hem something. Patch something. Replace a button. Photograph each one.
2. A summer or after-school job: a dry cleaner, a fabric store, a resale shop, a costume shop.
3. A high school CTE program in apparel, fashion, or design.
4. Then one of three: an apprenticeship, a certificate, or a degree, depending on which card you want.

- Start the folder today. Entry one is already done.

## Slide 16: Day 2. Review game: Make It, Mend It, Grab It

- Seven categories, six cards each.
- Cards 1 and 2 are worth 1 point. Cards 3 and 4 are worth 2. Cards 5 and 6 are worth 3.
- A station picks a category and a point value.
- I read the whole card. **Then** you get 20 seconds.
- Every station writes an answer. Every correct board scores.
- The picking station picks next.

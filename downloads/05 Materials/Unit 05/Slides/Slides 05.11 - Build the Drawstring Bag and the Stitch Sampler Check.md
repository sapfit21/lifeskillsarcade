# Slides 5.11: Build the Drawstring Bag, and the Stitch Sampler Check

Slide outline for Lesson 5.11, three days, eighteen slides. Slides 1 to 7 are Day 1 (cut, mark, pin, first seam). Slides 8 to 12 are Day 2 (second seam, the channel, the sampler scrap). Slides 13 to 18 are Day 3 (turn, cord, finish, and the check). The hand path is named on every work slide so the hand station never waits for an instruction.

## Slide 1 (Day 1): The build

- Lesson 5.11, day one of three
- A drawstring bag: cut to a pattern, machine seams, turned right side out, a channel, a cord
- Hand station: the felt pouch, hand backstitched, same checklist, same rubric
- Machines are threaded. I did it this morning. Today we sew.

## Slide 4: The pattern, one more time

- The seam allowance is a quarter inch; the needle plate has a guide line for it
- Right sides TOGETHER, inside out, because this bag gets turned
- The side seams STOP at the channel marks. The gap is not a mistake, it is the door the cord comes through.
Image: the pattern with the fold line, seam lines, and the shaded channel area labeled LEAVE OPEN HERE.

## Slide 5: Pins and machines

- Pins perpendicular to the seam, heads out
- Every pin comes OUT before it reaches the foot
- You never sew over a pin: a needle that hits a pin breaks
- And a broken needle is handed to me, both pieces, flat on your palm
Image: a pinned seam approaching a presser foot, a hand pulling the nearest pin.

## Slide 6: The first seam

- Edge on the quarter-inch guide
- Three or four reverse stitches at the start
- Slowest steady speed, hands to the sides, eyes just ahead of the needle
- Stop at the channel mark, reverse three or four, done
Image: a seam being sewn with the reverse lever circled.

## Slide 7 (Day 1 close): Count and close down

- Pins counted, "N pins in work" in the notes column
- Presser foot down, power off, chair in
- Exit line: what does the reverse lever do, and where did you use it?
Image: the count sheet's notes column reading "6 pins in work."

## Slide 8 (Day 2): Do now

- Needle down, foot up, pivot, foot down
- Where in today's sewing will you actually use that?
Image: the channel fold pinned around a bag top.

## Slide 9: Threading, for real this time

- Licensed threaders: thread your machine from nothing, buddy verifies
- Not licensed yet: I thread yours, and we finish your license this week
- The manual at your machine is still the authority
Image: the license line with two initials.

## Slide 10: The channel

- Fold the top edge down one inch toward the wrong side, all the way around
- Pin every two inches, one pass at my iron
- Sew along the bottom of the fold
- BOTH ENDS STAY OPEN. Both ends stay open.
Image: a channel seam circling a bag top with open gaps at both side seams, arrows pointing at the gaps.

## Slide 11: The sampler scrap

- Last four minutes: two scraps, one short seam, reverse-locked both ends
- Name on it in chalk
- This is Box 6 of your sampler card, sewn today so Thursday has time to score it
Image: the sampler card's Box 6, MACHINE SEAM.

## Slide 12 (Day 2 close): Count and close down

- Count, notes column, close-down routine

Image: a row of machines, presser feet down.

## Slide 13 (Day 3): Do now

- What still has to happen for your bag to be finished, and how long will it take?
- List the six things that go on your sampler card
Image: the six-box sampler card, empty.

## Slide 14: Turn, cord, finish

- Turn through the top; corners out with an eraser or a chopstick, never the shears
- Safety pin on the cord, feed the channel, knot the ends, pull: it closes
- Trim every thread end; three hand stitches close any gap
Image: a bag half turned right side out, and a safety pin leading a cord into a channel.

## Slide 15: The five-point self-check

- Cut to the pattern
- Seam on the line, both ends locked
- Even stitches
- Flat, not puckered
- Corners and ends closed
Image: the checklist from Handout 05.11, Part 4.

## Slide 16: Mount the sampler

- Six boxes: running stitch, backstitch, whipstitch, button, repair, machine seam
- Hand path: Box 6 is your best hand backstitch seam, labeled HAND PATH
- Label every box, run the self-check on each
Image: a finished sampler card, six boxes filled.

## Slide 17: The check, out loud

- Minute 20: machines closed down, tables cleared
- I score at your table, about 40 seconds each, out loud, so you hear the why
- The bag on the 20-point item rubric, the card on the 28-point check
Image: the scoring grid from Assessment 05.2.

## Slide 18 (Day 3 close): The reflection

- Back of the card, three lines: best at, would redo, can now make or fix
- Three read aloud at the bell
- Cards to the FACS folder. Bags go home. You built that.
Image: a hand pulling a drawstring bag closed.

# Teacher key

**Part 2, the tool table.**

| Tool | What it does | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Hand sewing needle | Carries the thread through the fabric | In the pin cushion, point in |
| Straight pin | Holds two pieces of fabric together before stitching | In the pin cushion |
| Pin cushion | Holds every needle and pin that is not in a hand | Center of the station table |
| Thread | The stitch itself; polyester all-purpose is the default | Kit box |
| Needle threader | Pulls the thread through the eye | Kit box, out in the open |
| Thimble | Protects the pushing finger on thick fabric | Kit box |
| Fabric shears | Cuts fabric. Nothing else, ever | Shears bin, blades down |
| Small snips | Cuts thread close to the fabric | Shears bin, blades down |
| Seam ripper | Opens a stitch line that went wrong | Kit box, cap on |
| Measuring tape | Measures fabric and a body | Kit box, rolled |
| Tailor's chalk | Marks the cut line and the seam line on fabric | Kit box |
| Paper scissors | Cuts paper patterns, so the fabric shears stay sharp | Kit box |
| Iron | Presses a fold or a seam flat | Front table, one operator, the teacher |

Needle sizes run backwards. A size 5 is a bigger, thicker needle than a size 9. Fabric shears cut fabric only, because paper dulls a blade in one cut, and a dull blade makes you push, and pushing is how people get hurt.

**Part 3, the six needle rules, in the wording used all unit.**

1. A needle or a pin lives in the pin cushion or the kit. Never on the table, never in your mouth, never in a pocket, never in the chair.
2. Count out, count in. No station is dismissed until the numbers match.
3. Snips and shears travel closed and pointing down, and they are handed over handle first.
4. Fabric shears cut fabric only.
5. One person operates the iron, hands away from the plate, cord behind and never across a walkway.
6. Your free hand stays behind the needle, never in front of it. If you get stuck, stop, tell the teacher, wash it, and it gets written up.

**The count-out and count-in routine, six steps.**

1. The Station Manager opens the kit and counts out loud, touching each one: needles, then pins.
2. The Station Manager writes both numbers in today's row on the station count sheet.
3. The station works. Any needle not in a hand is in the cushion.
4. At the five-minute warning, everything stops and every needle and pin goes back in the cushion.
5. The Station Manager counts again, out loud, and writes the second numbers.
6. The numbers match and the teacher initials the row, or the numbers do not match and nobody leaves that station until it is found.

**The machine close-down routine, on machine days, in the wording used all unit (canonical here and in Handout 05.10; change it here and there together, nowhere else).**

1. Presser foot down.
2. Power off.
3. Chair in.

A broken needle is reported and handed to the teacher, both pieces, flat on an open palm. It is never thrown out and never left on the floor.

**Part 4, the scenarios.**

| Scenario | Rule broken | What could happen | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Needle in the chair arm | Rule 1, and rule 2 if they leave before the count | The next person who sits in that chair or reaches for the arm gets stuck. A needle in upholstery disappears completely | Needle straight into the cushion, then ask to leave. The station is not done until the count is done |
| B. Shears handed blades first | Rule 3 | A cut hand, and a dropped pair of shears into a foot | Close them, hold the blades, and offer the handle. Or set them down on the table and let the other person pick them up |
| C. A count off by one and nobody stops | Rule 2 | A needle on the floor becomes a needle in a foot, a knee, or a hand, next period, for somebody who was never in this class | Nobody leaves the station. Search the table, chairs, work, floor, pockets, and cuffs. Then hand the sheet to the teacher and stand back for the magnet sweep |

Score Part 4 for three things per scenario: the rule named, a real consequence, and a correct action. This is Objective 3 of the lesson.

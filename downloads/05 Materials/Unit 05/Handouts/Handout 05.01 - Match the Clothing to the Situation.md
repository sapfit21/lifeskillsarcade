# Teacher key

Part 1 and Part 2 have many right answers. Score them complete, partial, or missing. The four jobs must all be filled in with a matching example; the example that students copy from the slide instead of writing their own is the one to push back on.

**Part 2, the expected answers:**

| Item | What it protects | Without it | Where required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apron | Clothing from spills, stains, and heat | Ruined clothes, hot liquid soaking through to skin | Kitchens, labs, art rooms, this room |
| Oven mitt | Hands and wrists from burns | A burn from a pan, a rack, or steam. A wet mitt is worse than no mitt, because steam goes right through | Any kitchen |
| Hair tied back or hair net | Food from hair, and hair from machines and flame | Hair in food, hair caught in a mixer, hair in a flame | Kitchens, food service, shops, labs |
| Closed-toe shoes | Feet from dropped, spilled, and sharp things | A dropped pan, a dropped iron, a dropped pair of shears, hot liquid on the foot | Kitchens, shops, hospitals, construction, this room |
| Safety glasses or goggles | Eyes from splashes and flying pieces | Chemical in the eye, a fragment in the eye | Science labs, shops, construction |
| Work glove | Hands from cuts, splinters, and rough material | Cuts and splinters. Also note: you cannot do fine work in one | Construction, landscaping, warehouses |
| High-visibility vest | The worker, by making them visible | Getting hit by a vehicle or machine that did not see them | Road crews, warehouses, parking lots, custodial work outdoors |
| Bike helmet | The head and brain in a fall | A head injury | Riding a bike, skating, scooters |

**Part 3, what counts as a Meets answer.** The outfit does not have to match any single "right" answer. It has to be defensible, include footwear, and the reason sentence has to name all three: weather, activity, setting. The most common failures and the correct pushes:

| Card | The usual mistake | The push |
|---|---|---|
| Job interview | No shoes named, or sneakers with no reasoning | "What do the shoes say in this setting?" |
| Construction site | Sneakers, shorts, no head protection | "What is overhead? What could be dropped on a foot?" A hard hat, long pants, and closed sturdy shoes are the point |
| Wedding | Turns into a fashion conversation | "What does the family expect of a guest?" Then add the grass and the 84 degrees, which rules out some footwear |
| Soccer game | Cotton shirt for a wet game | This previews Lesson 5.3: a wicking fabric beats cotton here, and that is the fiber lesson coming |
| Cold walk | One thick coat, no layers, no rain plan | "Three thin layers or one thick one? Which one lets you take something off when you get inside?" |

**Part 4, the sort.** Most rules belong in more than one box, and saying so is the point.

| Rule | Expected boxes |
|---|---|
| Restaurant kitchen | Safety (non-slip shoes, jewelry in machinery), Sanitation (hair, jewelry in food), Image (the customer sees the staff) |
| Hospital | Safety, Sanitation (nails and rings carry bacteria into a sterile room), Team identity (scrubs identify the role) |
| This school | Answers vary by code. Most school codes are Image and Team identity, with a Safety line for shop and lab classes. Do not lead students to a verdict on this school's code; the reasoning is the graded part |

The "what it costs" sentence earns credit for naming money, comfort, time, or self-expression. A blank or "nothing" gets one question at the desk: "What if the shoes cost sixty dollars and you only need them for work?"

# Teacher key

## Part 1, the measurements

There is no single right answer; the numbers belong to the student's item. What has to be true:

- The **cut size is larger than the finished size** on every edge that gets sewn, by the seam allowance. A half inch per sewn edge is the standard for this project, so a pouch finished at 8 by 4 inches, sewn on three edges, is cut at about 9 by 4.5 inches, plus whatever the fold or the top hem takes.
- The seam allowance is written as a number, not as "some."
- At least four measurements appear, in inches.

## Part 2, the order of work

A possible order, for reference, reading roughly: measure, mark, cut, fold, pin or clip, stitch the structural seams, attach the closure, finish the raw edges, knot and trim, test with the contents. Accept any order that can physically be done. The common impossible orders are: stitching a seam before cutting the piece out; attaching a flap or button before the body is sewn, so the student cannot reach inside; and finishing the top edge before threading a drawstring through it.

The step that cannot be undone is almost always the cut. A student who says "the cut" has the point.

## Part 3, the mock-up

Score on whether a change came out of it. The best mock-ups produce a measurement change (a channel that was too narrow, a sleeve that was an inch short after the seam, a flap that did not reach the button). A student who writes "it worked perfectly" either built something trivial or did not test it; send them back with one instruction, "test it with the real thing in it."

## Part 4, the approval table

This is the gate. Fabric shears stay at the teacher's table. Five questions, about sixty seconds, every student. The class list with the five tick boxes is the record and it feeds Rubric 05 criterion 1.

A student who cannot be fixed in a minute is moved to a simpler Easy path with the same material. That is written on the path change line, not on the rubric, and it does not lower the score.

## Part 5, the peer review

Scored complete, partial, or missing for daily work. A complete review has all five boxes checked yes or no, two specific "this will work because" lines, and one change with a reason. "It's good" and "I like it" are sent back once.

The student's own response, taking one change and declining another with a reason, is the part that matters. A student who takes every suggestion has not evaluated anything, and a student who takes none has not listened. Either one gets one question from the teacher.

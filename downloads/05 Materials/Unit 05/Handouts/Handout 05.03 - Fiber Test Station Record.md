# Teacher key

**Part 1a.** Natural: cotton, wool, silk, linen. Manufactured: polyester, nylon, rayon, spandex. Rayon is the one students miss; it starts as wood pulp, which is a plant, but it is processed into fiber in a factory, so it is counted as manufactured.

**Part 1b.** Cotton: a plant, the fluff around the seed of the cotton plant. Wool: an animal, the fleece of a sheep (other animal fibers include alpaca, mohair from goats, and cashmere). Silk: an insect, the thread a silkworm spins for its cocoon. Linen: a plant, the stem of the flax plant.

**Part 1c, expected answers.**

| Fiber | Warmth | Water | Wrinkles | Stretch | Cost and care |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cotton | Some | Absorbs a lot, dries slowly | Wrinkles | None on its own | Cheap; can shrink; machine wash |
| Wool | High, even when damp | Absorbs, dries slowly | Springs back, resists wrinkles | Some, springy | Costly; hand wash or dry clean; hot water felts it |
| Silk | Some | Absorbs; water can leave a mark | Wrinkles some | Little | Costly; hand wash or dry clean |
| Linen | Low, coolest in heat | Absorbs a lot, dries fast | Wrinkles the most | None | Costly; gets softer with age |
| Polyester | Some, traps heat | Beads or wicks, does not absorb, dries fast | Resists wrinkles | Little on its own | Cheap; machine wash; melts with heat; holds odor |
| Nylon | Some | Beads, dries fast | Resists wrinkles | Some | Cheap; strong; melts with heat |
| Rayon | Low | Absorbs, weak when wet | Wrinkles | Little | Mid cost; often hand wash |
| Spandex | Low | Beads | Resists wrinkles | High, springs back | Always blended; dryer heat destroys it |

**Part 2, the station set.** Label the swatches A to F in this order or your own; keep the answer envelope matched to the labels.

| Swatch | Fiber | What students should find |
|---|---|---|
| A | Cotton | Smooth to soft, cool, thin; no stretch; water soaks straight in |
| B | Wool | Rough or fuzzy, warm, thick; springs back a little; water beads at first then soaks slowly |
| C | Silk or linen | Silk: very smooth, cool, thin. Linen: slightly rough, cool, thicker slubs; both absorb |
| D | Polyester (fleece or shirting) | Smooth or fuzzy, warm, varies; little stretch; water beads and sits on top |
| E | Nylon (windbreaker or tights) | Slick, cool, thin; some stretch; water beads and runs off |
| F | Spandex blend (leggings) | Smooth, warm, thick; stretches a lot and comes back; water beads |

Score the station record complete, partial, or missing: complete means three tests filled for at least four swatches, with a real use named (not a color) for each.

**Part 3, the burn test results.** These are the standard identification results.

| Fiber | Flame | Smell | Residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cotton | Burns fast, keeps burning | Burning paper or leaves | Soft gray ash that crumbles |
| Wool | Burns slowly, curls away, often self-extinguishes | Burning hair | Black bead that crushes to powder |
| Polyester | Does not burn, melts and shrinks away, drips | Sweet and chemical | Hard bead that will not crush |

The melt sentence: because melted fiber sticks to skin and keeps burning it, so the burn is worse and it cannot be brushed off.

**Part 4b, defensible answers.** The fabric named matters less than the evidence.

| Use | Best choice and why |
|---|---|
| Winter hat | Wool (B). Warm even when damp, springs back, holds its shape. A fleece polyester (D) is also defensible: warm, cheap, dries fast |
| Soccer jersey | Polyester (D) or nylon (E). Water beads and moves away instead of soaking in, dries fast, light. Cotton is the wrong answer and the tests show why: it soaked the drop right in and stays wet |
| Dress shirt for a job interview | Cotton (A) for breathability and the look, or a cotton and polyester blend for wrinkle resistance. The reason has to come from the tests |
| Swimsuit | Spandex blend (F). Stretches and comes back, water beads, dries fast, and it has to move with a body |

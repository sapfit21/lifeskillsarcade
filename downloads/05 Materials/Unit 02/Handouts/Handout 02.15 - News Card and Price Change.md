## Part B (Day 2): Fact or hype?

**The three tests.** Score each headline: one point for each test it passes.
- **Who says it?** A named source (the venture, the Crown, a reporter) passes. "Insiders," "everyone," or no source fails.
- **Is there a number?** A percent, an amount, a date, or a count passes. No number fails.
- **Does it tell you to hurry?** No rush words passes. "Now," "last chance," "before it's too late," "everyone is buying" fails.

Two or three points: probably a fact. Zero or one: hype.

## Teacher key

**Part A matches:** 1 D; 2 A; 3 B; 4 G; 5 F; 6 E; 7 H; 8 C.

Sample cause and effect sentences (accept any that name the right direction and the right venture):
1. A huge crystal seam was found, so more people wanted to buy Sparkstone, so the price went up.
2. Hail destroyed crops, so more people wanted to sell Meadowgold, so the price went down.
3. The Crown promised an extra payment, so more people wanted to buy Crown Seal Notes, so the price went up (a little; safe ventures move a little).
4. The expedition found nothing and the company is almost out of coin, so more people wanted to sell Cloudberry, so the price went down (a lot; a young company with one voyage left is close to failing).
5. A frost shrank the harvest, so more people wanted to sell Willowbrook, so the price went down (a little; the trees are fine and the grove is built for the long run).
6. A rumor said the Vault lent badly, so some people wanted to sell the Iron Vault, so the price went down (a little; the Vault denied it and bonds move less).
7. A new road made the land more valuable, so more people wanted to buy Meadowgold, so the price went up.
8. The Honeypot pays the same no matter what, so nobody changed their mind, so the price did not move.

9. Card 2 is bad news for the land (crops lost; less rent) and card 7 is good news for the land (a road makes it worth more). Same venture, opposite direction, because the news changed what buyers expect the land to earn.
10. Enough people believed the rumor, or feared others would, to sell. A price is what people think today, not what is true. This is why the three tests matter.

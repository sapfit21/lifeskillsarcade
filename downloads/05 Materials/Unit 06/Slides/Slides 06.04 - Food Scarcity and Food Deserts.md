# Slides 6.4: Food Scarcity and Food Deserts

Slide outline for Lesson 6.4. Twelve slides, one day. Slide 2 is read out loud word for word and is not skipped. The last slide carries the counselor's name and room.

## Slide 1: Food Scarcity and Food Deserts

- Unit 6, Lesson 6.4
- Topic 6.1 Where Food Comes From
- Today: one neighborhood on paper, two price lists, twenty dollars
Image: a plain line drawing of a street grid with one store icon at the far corner.

## Slide 2: Three rules for today

- Nobody is asked about their own household. Not your fridge, not your groceries, not anything
- This is not something that happens only somewhere else. About one in eight households in this country has trouble affording enough food in a given year
- We are here to solve a problem, not to feel sorry for anybody
Notes: Read all three out loud, word for word. Do not paraphrase and do not skip. The lesson only works if the room knows it is safe.

## Slide 3: Do now

- The words "food desert" describe some neighborhoods
- From the words alone, what do you think it means?
- One sentence. There is no wrong guess here
Notes: Take four guesses and correct none of them. Most will say "a place with no food." That guess is the opening of the lesson.

## Slide 4: There is plenty of food for sale here

- A store on almost every corner
- Open late, open early, some open all night
- And it is still hard to put a decent dinner on the table
- Your job: figure out why
Notes: This is the hook.

## Slide 5: Two words

- **Food insecurity:** not having reliable access to enough affordable food
- **Food desert:** a neighborhood where reaching a full grocery store is hard because of distance, transportation, or both
Notes: Students write both in their own words. Watch for "a place with no food" on the handout and fix it now, not later.

## Slide 6: Four things that create one

- Distance: how far the nearest full grocery store is
- Transportation: whether you can get there and back with groceries
- Price: what the nearby stores charge
- Selection: what the nearby stores actually stock
Image: four icons, a ruler, a bus, a price tag, an empty shelf.

## Slide 7: Maple Park

- 4,000 people, twelve blocks
- One supermarket, three convenience stores
- One bus, once an hour
- No sidewalk on Route 9 between Elm and the store
Image: the neighborhood map from the handout, projected large, with the scale bar visible.
Notes: Made up for class. Say that out loud.

## Slide 8: Measure it

- Three homes, six distances
- 1 inch equals 0.4 miles. One block is about 0.1 miles
- Highlight the walk from Home C to the supermarket
- Then: who has the hardest trip, and what would make it easier?
Notes: The fix has to be a change to the neighborhood, not to the family. If a student says "they should get a car," that is the discussion, not the answer.

## Slide 9: Twenty dollars, two stores

- Build a dinner for a family of four
- First at the supermarket, then at the corner store
- Both carts have to feed four people
- Then: what is the difference, in dollars and in food?
Notes: Circulate. Any cart with fresh vegetables from the corner store gets sent back to the price list, not corrected out loud.

## Slide 10: The item that is not on the list at all

- No fresh vegetables
- No fresh meat
- One banana, ninety-nine cents
Notes: This is the slide students remember. It is not only that the corner store is expensive. It is that some food is not for sale there at any price.

## Slide 11: Five responses

- Food pantry: free groceries, often one day a week
- SNAP and WIC: federal programs, run through the state
- Community garden: neighbors grow food on a shared plot
- Mobile market: a truck that parks in the neighborhood on a schedule
- School meals: breakfast and lunch, at no charge in many New York schools
Notes: One minute each, factual. What each one is, who runs it, what it does and does not solve. Then: which two would help Maple Park most, and why.

## Slide 12: Exit card, and my door

- A food desert is ___
- The thing that would help most is ___ because ___
- If anything today is about your house, my door and the counselor's door are both open, and you do not have to explain
Notes: Read the last line out loud. Read the exit cards before you hand anything back.

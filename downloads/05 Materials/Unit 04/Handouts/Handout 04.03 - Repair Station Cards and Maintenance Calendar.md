## Day 2: The station cards

Four minutes per station. Everyone performs once. Teammates coach. Initial the box when you pass the test.

### Station 1: Drive a screw

Safety: safety glasses on. Fingers behind the screw, never in front of the tip.
1. Look at the screw head. Plus sign means Phillips. Flat slot means flathead. Pick the driver that fills the head.
2. Set the screw tip in a pilot hole. Hold it straight.
3. Push in and turn clockwise (righty tighty). Keep pushing so the driver does not slip.
4. Stop when the head is flush with the wood. Do not keep going; that strips it.
5. Now take it out: turn counterclockwise (lefty loosey).
**Test:** the head sat flush and the driver never slipped out. My initials: ______

### Station 2: Plunge a drain

Safety: hands dry before you touch anything else. Never plunge a drain that has had chemicals in it.
1. Put the cup of the plunger over the drain so it covers it completely.
2. Make sure there is enough water to cover the cup. The seal needs water.
3. Push down slowly to push the air out, then push and pull straight up and down six times, hard.
4. Pull the plunger off. In a real drain, run hot water to see if it flows.
**Test:** you felt the seal grab and the push-pull got harder, not easier. My initials: ______

### Station 3: Read a tape measure

Safety: let the tape back in slowly. It snaps.
1. Hook the end on the edge of the thing you are measuring, or line it up with the start of the line.
2. Read the big numbers for inches. The longest marks between numbers are half inches. The next longest are quarters. The next are eighths.
3. Write each measurement to the nearest eighth of an inch.

### Station 4: Use a level and hang a frame

Safety: the level is a tool, not a sword. Two hands.
1. Set the level on top of the bracket. Watch the bubble.
2. Pivot the bracket until the bubble sits exactly between the two lines.
3. Mark the second screw hole with a pencil.
4. Now the frame: hang it on the nail, set the level on top, nudge until the bubble is centered.
**Test:** bubble between the lines on both. My initials: ______

### Station 5: The breaker and the bulb

Safety: nothing at this station is live. At home, a breaker panel is the one place you never put a wet hand or a tool.
1. Find the tripped breaker in the photo. It is the one sitting in the middle, not fully ON and not fully OFF.
2. Say the reset in order: push it fully OFF first, then push it ON.
3. If it trips again right away: stop. Unplug what you just turned on and tell an adult. Something is drawing too much.
4. The lamp: the switch is off. Is the bulb cool? Read the label on the socket: max ______ watts. Turn the old bulb out counterclockwise. Turn the new LED in clockwise until snug, not tight.
**Test:** you said "off, then on" in that order, and the new bulb is snug. My initials: ______

## Teacher key

**Part 1** (accept close wording):

| Job | First step | Safety rule | Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light bulb | Switch off | Let it cool; check the max watts on the fixture | Hands, a step stool that is not cracked |
| Breaker | Find the one in the middle | Fully off, then on; if it trips again, stop | Hands only, dry |
| Drain | Pull out hair with a plastic drain tool | Never mix chemicals; never plunge after chemicals | Drain tool, plunger |
| Running toilet | Lift the tank lid | Turn off the water at the valve if it overflows | Hands; a screwdriver for the float screw |
| Loose screw | Match the driver to the head | Push in while turning; stop when snug | Screwdriver |
| Hang on a wall | Find the stud or use an anchor | Mark with a level; avoid where wires or pipes run | Stud finder, level, hammer or driver |
| Filter | Find the arrow (points toward the furnace) | Switch the system off first | Hands |

**Part 2:** Phillips screwdriver (tighten a screw); flathead screwdriver (tighten a screw; pry a paint can); pliers (grip, bend, pull a nail, hold a nut); adjustable wrench (tighten a nut or a bolt, a faucet fitting); level (hang something straight, level a shelf); tape measure (measure a space or a piece before buying or cutting); plunger (unclog a drain; the toilet plunger has the flange).

**Part 3:** a. name what is wrong; b. name what it should do; c. test to find the cause; d. fix it; e. check that the fix worked; f. stop it happening again. Toilet: keeps running; should stop within a minute; check the chain and the flapper; fix by freeing the chain or reseating the flapper or lowering the float; flush and time it; check the flapper yearly, replace it every few years ($8). Safety rules: eyes protected at the screw station; no tool leaves its station; nothing is plugged in; dry hands at the drain station; one person works while the team coaches.

**Part 4** (accept any month that fits the season; the reasoning is what is scored): detectors monthly, every month; furnace filter every one to three months; range hood monthly; lint trap every load, dryer vent yearly (fall); batteries yearly (many families use the fall clock change); gutters in late fall after the leaves and again in spring; furnace check in September or October; fans in October and April; hoses in October or November before the first freeze; salt in December to February; roof in March or April; AC in May; deck in April or May; water heater any month, once; extinguisher gauge any month, once; ten-year detectors when the date on the back says so.

Three placements: for example, gutters in November because the leaves have fallen; hoses in October because a frozen hose can crack the pipe in the wall; AC in May because you want to find out it is broken before July.

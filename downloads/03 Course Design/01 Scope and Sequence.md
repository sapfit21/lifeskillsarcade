## Why this order

1. Kitchen first. Sal's own reflection: the first cooking lab is what won the class. Safety and sanitation come before any lab, so Unit 1 opens with the safety contract and reaches a first lab by day 9.
2. Finance and careers second, before winter break. His strongest content and his own simulation, when the class already trusts him.
3. Human development in the middle of the year, when peer dynamics in the room are visible and worth talking about.
4. Design and apparel back to back: both use the elements and principles of design, so the second builds on the first.
5. Food systems late, after students already have kitchen skills, so the labs can be bigger (four kitchens at once) and the thinking can go beyond the recipe.
6. A capstone that shows your school what is different about this teacher: a resume, an interview, and a pitch.

Threads that run all year: careers (every module has a Career Pathways topic; every unit has at least one careers day), consumer and financial literacy (every content module except HDR has a Consumer Resources and Finance topic), safety (Health, Safety, and Wellness theme topic 4), communication and teamwork (roles in every lab and project), and decision making (the five-step model from Unit 0 comes back in every project).

## Unit 0: Welcome to the FACS Room (5 days)

Purpose: routines, trust, teamwork, and the decision-making model the whole year uses. No content module; themes carried: Communication and Interpersonal Relationships, Problem Solving and Innovation, Health, Safety, and Wellness.

| Day | Topic | What students do | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is FACS and who is in this room | Do now on the board; course tour; the six modules as a year map; "what I hope we cook" card | Exit card |
| 2 | How this room works | Room and lab tour, zones, the reset cue, role cards explained; Draw/See/Hear communication game | Observation checklist |
| 3 | Decisions, step by step | Plane Crash decision simulation: rank survival items alone, then as a team; the five-step decision model | Team ranking sheet |
| 4 | Teamwork under pressure | Silent Measurement Challenge preview (no ingredients yet: water and rice); team norms written | Team contract |
| 5 | The agreement | Room and Lab Agreement read, discussed, signed; pre-assessment quiz on food safety and nutrition (for Unit 1) | Signed agreement, pre-assessment |

## Unit 1: Kitchen Skills and Smart Eating (30 days)

Module: Nutrition and Wellness (N&W). Themes: Health, Safety, and Wellness (primary), Financial and Consumer Literacy, Problem Solving and Innovation, Communication and Interpersonal Relationships, Sustainability (lab waste), Career and Community Opportunities (careers day).

| Topic | Days | Lessons (a lesson can span days) | Formal check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Safe and Clean: Kitchen Safety and Sanitation | 5 | 1.1 Hazards in a kitchen (hunt the hazard); 1.2 Handwashing, the danger zone, cross contamination; 1.3 Fire, burns, cuts, and what to do; 1.4 Write the class Kitchen Safety Contract | Kitchen Safety Exam (must pass to cook) |
| 1.2 Tools, Measuring, and Recipes | 7 | 1.5 Tools and equipment and what each is for; 1.6 Measuring dry, liquid, and small amounts (Silent Measurement Challenge); 1.7 Reading a recipe: yield, abbreviations, steps, doubling and halving; 1.8 First lab: Mug Cake with hand-whipped cream (grade 6 version) or Scrambled Egg Tacos | Tools and Measuring performance check; Lab 1 rubric |
| 1.3 Nutrients, MyPlate, and Labels | 7 | 1.9 The six nutrients and what they do (pre-assessed; coordinate with health); 1.10 MyPlate beyond the food groups: portions and a real plate; 1.11 Reading a Nutrition Facts label (Label Detective); 1.12 Is this food video true? Credible sources; 1.13 Lab 2: Sheet Pan Nachos with a label discussion while it bakes | Label Reading Quiz; Lab 2 rubric |
| 1.4 Plan, Shop, Cook: Meals on a Budget | 11 | 1.14 Planning a meal for a family: needs, likes, cost; 1.15 Shopping lists and store circulars; 1.16 Unit price and comparison shopping; 1.17 Storing food safely and reducing waste; 1.18 Project launch: Feed a Family of Four on $40 (plan and price); 1.19 Peer math check and MyPlate check; 1.20 Budget Meal Lab (cook one dish from the plan); 1.21 Menu card, pitch, and tasting gallery; 1.22 Careers in nutrition and culinary arts (careers day) | Project rubric (plan, math, nutrition, lab, pitch); Unit 1 test |

No-kitchen alternative for every lab: a measuring and recipe-scaling station lab with dry goods and water, a no-cook recipe (overnight oats, trail mix, salsa with pre-cut vegetables), or a paper "plate build" with food cards.

## Flex versions

- 30 weeks daily (150 days): drop Unit 7 (fold the resume and STAR lessons into Unit 2 Topic 2.5, adding 3 days), cut Unit 1 to 26, Unit 2 to 26, Unit 3 to 22, Unit 4 to 21, Unit 5 to 24, Unit 6 to 21 by removing the marked flex lessons (listed in each unit plan). Total 5 + 26 + 26 + 22 + 21 + 24 + 21 + 5 (careers and STAR) = 150.
- One semester daily (90 days): Unit 0 (3), Unit 1 (20: safety, tools, one lab, labels, the $40 project), Unit 2 (20: who I am, money basics, a 10-day ClassroomStreet run), Unit 3 (12: lifespan, stress, communication, babysitting basics), Unit 4 (10: safety and conservation, color, a floor plan), Unit 5 (15: fibers and care, hand sewing, a small item), Unit 6 (10: where food comes from, one lab, a scaled shopping task). Every module still appears.
- Every other day for a year (90 meetings): same as the semester plan, with the ClassroomStreet trading windows and the daily floor plan do now run as homework or on the off days.
- Two-year spiral (if students take FACS in both grade 7 and grade 8): Year A = Units 0, 1, 2, 3; Year B = Units 4, 5, 6, 7, with Unit 1's safety exam repeated at the start of Year B.

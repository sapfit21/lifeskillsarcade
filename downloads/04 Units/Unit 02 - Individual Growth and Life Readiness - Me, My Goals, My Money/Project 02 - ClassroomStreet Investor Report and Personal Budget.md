## The scenario

You are a new merchant on ClassroomStreet with AQ25,000 you did not have yesterday. For ten class days you will buy and sell seven ventures, read the news, live through a crash, and find out on Day 9 what you were really buying. Then you write the Investor Report: what you did, why, what it cost or earned, and what it changed about your own budget.

The leaderboard is real and I will read the top five. The grade is not the leaderboard. The grade is whether you can explain your decisions with numbers, whether you kept your head in the crisis, and whether you took what you learned back to your own money.

## Round 2: trading with the truth

After the reveal, the market runs three more days for ten minutes each inside the career lessons. Same account, same rules, same leaderboard. The one difference is you: you now know that Sparkstone is a tech stock and the Honeypot is a savings account. Round 2 has its own small leaderboard: the last window closes at the reset cue in Lesson 2.22, and the standings go up by merchant number at the start of the next class. It is not on the rubric; the three Round 2 journal lines count as daily work, and nothing in Round 2 changes the report you already handed in. It is your chance to find out whether knowing the truth changes what you do.

## Team debriefs

Your portfolio and your report are yours alone. Three times you work with others:
- **Pairs** for the News Card match (Day 3), the fact or hype sort (Day 4), the Three Portfolios math (Day 5), the Part C comparison (Day 8), and the peer read (Day 10). A partner checks your work; a partner does not trade for you.
- **Four corners** on Day 7: everyone who sold some, sold everything, held, or bought more stands together and says why. Nobody's number is read unless they read it.
- **The class debrief** on Day 9: three merchant paths, by number only, and volunteers.

## Where you get to choose

- **What you buy and when.** Every trade in ten days is yours. The only rules are the Day 1 rules (three ventures, AQ1,000 cash) and the Day 6 rule (a written decision on the grid before you trade in the crisis).
- **Sell, hold, or buy** in the crisis. All three can be right. The grid is where you show me your reason.
- **The three decisions** in your report. Pick the three you can explain best, not the three that look best.
- **How you write the report.** Handwritten on the template, typed, dictated to a partner and checked, or recorded as a voice note with the numbers written on the template. The seven parts are the same in every form.
- **The savings line.** Add one, raise one, or split one into an emergency cushion and a long-term line. Your budget, your number, your reason.

# The paper ledger version (no devices)

If the site is down, the Wi-Fi is down, or the class has no devices, the whole simulation runs on paper with the same ten days, the same lessons, and the same report. The teacher is the market.

**What you need.** `Handout 02.19 - Paper Ledger.md` (one ledger sheet per student, plus the teacher key with the price table and the dispatch script), the board, and a calculator per pair.

**How a day runs.**
1. The teacher reads the day's dispatch aloud from the script (twice, once slow).
2. The teacher posts the day's seven prices on the board from the price table.
3. Students update the "price now" column and the "value" column on their ledger sheet (units held times price now), and total their fortune (cash plus all values).
4. Trading window: students write each trade on the ledger (ticker, buy or sell, units, price, cash in or out), then update cash and units. Whole units only. Cash may not go below zero.
5. The teacher collects the sheets at the bell every day, or students keep them in the folder if the class can be trusted with its own prices.

**The crisis on paper.** Day 6 prices fall hard for SPRK and CLDB and a little for MDOW; SEAL and HNYP hold; the dispatch says why. Day 7 falls further. Day 8 recovers. The four-corners poll, the decision grid, and Return Math all work from the sheet.

**The leaderboard on paper.** The teacher (or two student clerks, by merchant number only) lists total fortune at the end of Day 8 on the board for the closing bell on Day 9.

**Round 2 on paper.** Days 10 to 12 prices are in the price table. Same routine, ten minutes.

**What is lost and what is not.** Lost: the live ticker, the between-window suspense, and the site's own ledger export (the paper ledger is the export). Not lost: every decision, every number in the report, and every criterion on the rubric.

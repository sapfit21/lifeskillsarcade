## How to use this rubric

- Hand the rubric out with the project sheet in Lesson 2.14 Day 1 so students trade against it. Nothing on it should be a surprise; say on Day 1 that the leaderboard is not a criterion.
- The student fills the self-assessment row at the end of Lesson 2.19 Day 2, before the teacher scores. The evidence sentence must contain a number or an entry number.
- Criterion 1 is scored from two things together: the ledger export from the Command Bridge (trades or, for a hold, the journal entry that says "held" with a reason) and the journal count. A student who held all week with a written reason every day meets criterion 1; holding is a decision. In the paper ledger version, the ledger sheet is the export. The three Round 2 lines (Lessons 2.20 to 2.22) come after the report is scored; they count as daily work, not on this rubric.
- Criterion 3 is scored from the decision grid first and the report's part 4 second. Score the reasoning, not the outcome. A student who sold everything at the bottom with a completed grid and an honest look back can score a 4; a student who held by accident because they forgot to log in cannot score above a 2.
- Criteria 2 and 4 are scored on the report as submitted after the peer read. Criterion 5 is scored on part 7 with the Lesson 2.9 budget attached for comparison.
- Every descriptor is written as what the student did. If a report does not match any box, pick the closest and write why in the notes line.
- My original 16-point rubric had four criteria: participation, analysis and decision making, emotional control, and reflection quality. They are criteria 1, 2, 3, and 4 here, with the descriptors rewritten as observable actions. Criterion 5 is new and is what ties the simulation back to the student's own money.
- The rubric goes home with the report. Students keep the self-assessment in the FACS folder.

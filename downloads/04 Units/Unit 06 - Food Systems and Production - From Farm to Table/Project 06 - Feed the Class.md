## The scenario

There are twenty-eight people in this room and I have about eighty dollars. In eight class periods, you are going to feed everybody one real meal, and I am not going to plan it, price it, or cook it for you. You are the kitchen.

Your station picks a dish. You scale the recipe from four servings up to the whole class. You price it, to the penny, from this week's real supermarket circular. You write your own flowchart in five-minute blocks and your own station card. You hand me one shopping list for the whole class and I buy exactly what is on it. Then you cook it, serve it to everybody else before you serve yourself, clean the room to a signed checklist, and tell me what it actually cost and what you would change.

I have done this before. Four kitchens in one period, 112 students across four periods on a $150 budget, every price checked against the store circular. It worked because the plan was on paper before anybody touched a burner. That is the part you own.

## The budget

- **$20.00 per station.** That is what your station gets to spend on its own dish, for 28 portions plus two spares.
- **$80.00 for the class.** Four stations, $20 each. If your station comes in under, that is a good result, not a missed chance. Coming in under is what a real kitchen tries to do.
- **Cost per person** is the number you will report twice: what you planned in Lesson 6.15, and what the receipt actually said in Lesson 6.17.

All prices in the handouts are real Long Island supermarket prices to be refreshed from the current circular before teaching. The math works no matter what the prices are; only the answers change.

## The constraints (the constraint card, and none of these are negotiable)

| # | Constraint | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No knives. Butter knives and kitchen shears only, and only if your card says so | Chopping, slicing, dicing. Anything that needs cutting comes pre-cut, pre-shredded, or is torn or pulled apart with two forks |
| 2 | No frying | Cutlets, anything in a pan of oil, anything in a fryer |
| 3 | Pre-cooked meat only, reheated to 165 F, or no meat | Raw chicken, raw ground beef, raw fish, raw eggs as an ingredient you handle |
| 4 | Stovetop, oven, and microwave only | Grills, blenders, food processors, anything the room does not own |
| 5 | The posted class allergy list | Every item on the list, unless you write the substitution on your card |
| 6 | 40 minutes, and the class has to eat inside the period | Anything that bakes for 45 minutes. Anything that has to rest for an hour |
| 7 | $20.00 per station | Steak, shrimp, fresh berries in May, anything you cannot cover for 30 portions |

**How a team picks.** In Lesson 6.12 your station walks all five cards, fills the comparison table, then ranks its top two on a slip. If two stations both want the oven, the flowchart in Lesson 6.14 is where you solve it, not here.

## Where you get to choose

- **The dish.** Five options, and your station's rankings decide. A station may also propose a sixth option of its own, priced from the circular, for teacher approval.
- **The swaps.** Any store-brand or size swap that saves at least twenty-five cents and does not break the allergy list or the station card is yours to make.
- **The flowchart.** Nobody hands you the schedule. You build it, and you decide which shortcut to take if you run late.
- **The station card format.** Written or drawn. A picture flowchart card with eight boxes counts as a real card if a substitute could follow it.
- **The reflection format.** Written, typed, dictated to a teammate, recorded as a voice note with the numbers on paper, or drawn as a marked-up flowchart. Four questions, any form.
- **The table.** In Lesson 6.17 the host's opening sentence at your table can be in any language you speak.

## How it is scored

`Rubric 06 - Feed the Class.md`, five criteria, each Not Yet (1), Approaching (2), Meets (3), or Exceeds (4), for 20 points. Your team fills the self-assessment row before I score. Criteria 1, 2, 3, and 5 are team scores; criterion 4 (the cook day) has a team part and an individual part. A safety violation on cook day is an individual zero for that day's participation under the grading plan, is a conversation and a redo of the clean phase rather than a lecture, and caps the team at Approaching on criterion 4 for that day.

The cook day itself is also scored individually on `Rubric 01 - Lab Rubric.md` (16 points), which is the lab rubric used for every lab this year. You have both rubrics in hand from day one of the project. Nothing on either one should be a surprise.

The project counts in the labs and projects category, 40 percent of the marking period. A late project is accepted with a one-level drop on one criterion, never a zero.

# The no-kitchen version

Everything in this project except the heat is unchanged. If the room has no kitchen, or the stove is down, or the class is in a regular classroom, the meal becomes a no-cook assembled meal and the project runs exactly the same eight days with the same six deliverables, the same rubric, and the same budget.

**What changes:** the five menu options become five assembly options, the flowchart phases become setup, portion, assemble, serve and eat, clean, and the critical path becomes whichever item has to be opened, drained, brought from a cooler, or set out first. Nothing else changes. Scaling, unit conversion, the yield check, costing from the circular, the swap log, the consolidated list, the twelve-item approval checklist, the station card, the pre-flight, the duty list, the serve-others-first rule, the four clean-up zones, the station check, and the reflection all stay.

**The no-kitchen constraint card** replaces items 3 and 4 with: no heat of any kind; the teacher opens every can and every sealed package; cold items travel in a cooler with ice packs and go out at minute 5, not minute 0; anything perishable that is not eaten goes in the trash at the bell, not back in a bag.

**What is lost, and what is gained.** The class does not demonstrate FSP 1. e) (prepare a simple recipe) with heat, and the cooking method content from Topic 6.2 does not get a second performance. Everything about the system, the math, the money, the plan, the service, the clean, and the reflection is identical, and the no-kitchen version is actually harder on the cold-chain and hold-time side, which is a real food safety lesson the kitchen version does not teach as well. Say that out loud to the class so the no-kitchen version does not read as the consolation prize.

# Teacher notes on the project

- This is Sal's own King Kullen multi-kitchen system, run by the students instead of by him. His version: six costed menus for 165 students across six periods and three kitchens on a $200 budget; an eleven-item shopping list for 112 students across four periods and four kitchens on $150 with store-brand swaps that saved $30.38; a one-page master flowchart in five-minute blocks marked substitute-teacher friendly; and one station card per kitchen for seven students with ingredients, ten numbered steps, safety reminders, and a "what does done look like" checklist. All four documents are in `Desktop/Education/TeachingWork/LessonPlans/`. The pedagogical move in this unit is that he planned all of it there and the students plan all of it here.
- The teacher's job in this project is to hold the constraints and buy the food. Everything else belongs to the students. The hardest part for a teacher who has done this before is not fixing the over-budget station's problem for them.
- Buy the day before cook day, not the same morning. Portion into four labeled station bins before the bell.
- One section or several: Sal's own system ran the same menu across six periods on one order, which is the version that scales. If two sections cook different menus, you buy and set up twice. Decide before Lesson 6.12, because it changes which options are on the table.
- Budget reality check: $80 per section per year for this project, plus about $8 in paper goods if the room does not already have them. Across four sections that is about $350. That is the single biggest line in the whole course. If the budget will only fund half, three stations instead of four with the two cheapest options brings a section to about $55.
- The three checkpoint signatures are what keep this project from going wrong: the costing sheet signed in 6.13, the station card approved in 6.14, and the consolidated list approved in 6.15. No signature, no next step. Say that on day one.
- The reflection is the part most likely to get cut for time, and it is the part that makes this a project instead of a cooking day. Protect the eight minutes in Lesson 6.17.

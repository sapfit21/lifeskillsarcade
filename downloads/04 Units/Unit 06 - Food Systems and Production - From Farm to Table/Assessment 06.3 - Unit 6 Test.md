**14.** (2 points: 1 for what it is, 1 for why) What it is: the one step that everything else in the plan waits on; the step that, if it is late, makes everything after it late. **15.** (2 points: 1 for the definition, 1 for the response) A food desert is an area where people do not have reasonable access to a store that sells enough affordable, fresh, healthy food, often because the nearest full grocery store is too far away or there is no way to get there. Accept any of these for the second point: a farmers market or mobile market in the neighborhood, a community garden, a food pantry, better bus service to a supermarket, a store that accepts SNAP, a school or church distribution, a corner store program that stocks fresh produce. ## Part F. Scenario (6 points)

**19.**
a. (1 point) **Preheating the oven.** Nothing else at this station can happen until the oven is at temperature. Accept "the oven getting to 400."
b. (2 points) Oven is on at minute 14, ready at minute 22 (14 + 8). Trays go in at 22, bake 10 minutes, out at **minute 32**. That is 3 minutes after plates were supposed to go down. Give 1 point for the correct addition to minute 22 and 1 point for minute 32. Accept a correct answer that uses a different bake time if the student states it.
c. (2 points, 1 per fix) Any two of: split the batch onto more trays or into the toaster oven so it bakes faster; bake at a slightly higher temperature and watch it; drop the last optional step (a second cheese layer, a garnish) and serve on time; serve the side or the dessert station's dish first while the trays finish and have the Head Cook bring the tortillas to the tables during the eat block; ask the teacher to open and load the oven the second it hits temperature so no minute is wasted. Do not accept "work faster" or "hope it cooks quicker." Do not accept anything that breaks a constraint (frying them, using a knife, serving them raw).
d. (1 point) **The clock is not negotiable, the presentation is.** A dish served plain and on time beats a perfect dish nobody eats. Plates go down at minute 29 whatever state the dish is in.
Standard: NYS FSP 5. d) (as above); CTE PSI 1. Problem Solving e) Demonstrate personal development of problem-solving skills through practice of these skills in a variety of classroom applications; CDOS 3a Universal Foundation Skills: Thinking Skills.

## Notes for the teacher

- Twenty minutes is the real time. Do not shorten it. A student who needs longer finishes in a support period or at the start of Unit 7.
- Items 16 and 17 are the two that decide whether this unit's math landed. If the class average on those two is below 4 out of 6, reteach the round-up rule and the price-per-ounce division in the first ten minutes of Unit 7 Lesson 7.1 as a do now, because Unit 7's pricing lesson depends on both.
- Item 16 part c is the one students miss most, because 3.5 looks like the answer. The round-up rule is the whole point of that item.
- Item 19 is the item that shows whether the flowchart work transferred. A student who names preheating as the critical path has understood the unit's single biggest idea.
- Item 20 is scored on the five lines, not on grammar. A student who writes it as a bulleted list with the five elements present earns full credit; say that in the directions if the class needs to hear it.
- Do not pre-teach the test. The review game on Day 2 uses the same four topic piles and the room's own walls, which is enough.

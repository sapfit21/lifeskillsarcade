## The scenario

You have been asked to babysit a child on Saturday afternoon. The parent says the child gets bored fast. You are going to show up with something in your bag that no other babysitter brings: one activity you designed for exactly this child's age, safe enough that the parent does not have to worry, cheap enough that you made it for under $5, and good enough that the child learns something while playing.

That is the kit. You will plan it, build it, write a card so the parent knows how to use it, check it for safety twice, test it with a partner playing the child, and show it at the Family Showcase.

## Step 1: Pick your child

Choose one age: 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6. The ages-and-stages chart on `Handout 03.18` tells you what a child that age is working on in each area:

- Physical: big movements (run, hop, climb) and small movements (pinch, stack, draw)
- Social: playing next to others, then with others; taking turns; sharing
- Emotional: naming feelings, handling "no," waiting
- Intellectual: words, colors, shapes, counting, sorting, why questions, pretend

Pick one area and one thing the child is working on. Your kit builds that one thing. Not four things. One.

## Step 2: Pick a kit type from the menu

| Kit type | What it is | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory bag | A sealed zip-top bag with gel or soap inside and things to find or push around (buttons, pom-poms, cut shapes) | Ages 2 to 4; physical (small movements) or intellectual (find the red one) | Double-bag and tape every edge. Nothing loose outside the bag for ages 2 to 3. |
| Story with props | A short story you tell (five to eight sentences) with three or four objects the child holds or moves as the story goes | Ages 3 to 6; intellectual (words, order) or emotional (a story about being scared, being brave) | Props must be big for ages 2 to 3. Write the story on the card so the parent can tell it. |
| Sorting game | A set of things to sort by color, size, or shape, with cups or a plate divided into sections | Ages 3 to 5; intellectual (matching, categories) | Small pieces are choking hazards for ages 2 to 3. For 4 to 6, "with an adult" goes on the card. |
| Movement game | A path, a set of cards (hop, spin, crawl, freeze), a follow-the-leader game, a beanbag toss into a box | Ages 2 to 6; physical (big movements) or social (turns) | Nothing to climb. Nothing that has to be thrown at a person. Show where to play it (a clear floor). |
| Simple craft | A paper plate animal, a yarn-wrapped stick, a torn-paper picture, a cup with a face | Ages 3 to 6; physical (small movements) or emotional (making a face that shows a feeling) | Child-safe scissors only, and only for ages 5 and 6 with an adult. Glue sticks, not liquid glue, for ages 2 to 4. |

## Step 3: The rules

Criteria (what the kit must do):
1. Fit the age exactly. A 3 year old's kit should bore a 6 year old and confuse a 2 year old.
2. Build one development area, and you can say which and why.
3. Be safe: pass every line on the safety checklist, twice.
4. Cost under $5 from the bin price list.
5. Come with a parent card a parent can read in one minute.

Constraints (what you have to work inside):
- Bin materials only. Nothing from home unless I approve it.
- No food in any kit.
- The tube rule: for ages 2 and 3, nothing loose that fits through a toilet paper tube. For ages 4 to 6, small pieces are allowed only if the card says "with an adult."
- Built in one class period.
- Nothing sharp, nothing that has to be plugged in, nothing that goes in a mouth.

## The Family Showcase

The Family Showcase is the Day 3 demo round. Kits sit on the counter under age signs (2, 3, 4, 5, 6) with the parent card in front of each. The tradition cards from Lesson 3.15 are posted on the wall above (name optional). Each student or pair gives a two-minute demo at their kit. Everyone else walks the showcase with a gallery card. If the room has a hallway case, the kits and cards stay up for the rest of the unit.

## Where you get to choose

- **The age.** Any of five.
- **The area and the one thing.** From the chart, your call.
- **The kit type.** Any of five, as long as it fits the age.
- **Alone or with a partner.** Either. A pair makes one kit and each person gives half the demo.
- **The parent card.** Written, or a picture card (draw the three steps and label each with one word), or both, or in two languages.
- **The demo.** At your kit for the class, or at the table for me only.

## How it is scored

Rubric 03 has five criteria, each scored Not Yet (1), Approaching (2), Meets (3), or Exceeds (4), for 20 points. You fill the self-assessment row first with one sentence of evidence per criterion; then I score. A pair gets one score for criteria 1 to 4 and each person gets their own score for criterion 5 (the demo).

The project counts in the labs and projects category (40 percent of the marking period). A late project is accepted with a one-level drop on one criterion, never a zero. A kit that fails a safety line at the showcase (a loose piece that fits through the tube for a 2 or 3 year old kit) is capped at Approaching on criterion 2 until it is fixed, and it can be fixed the same day.

# Teacher notes on the project

- This project is the state module's own two illustrative activities ("Activities for Afterschool Program" and "Prototype Toys") sized to a 40-minute schedule and a middle school room with no children in it. The test is a partner playing the age; no real child is observed or used.
- The Family Showcase is the Day 3 demo round, not a separate event. The tradition cards from Lesson 3.15 give the wall behind the kits a second layer (HDR 5. d)) at no extra time.
- Budget for the unit plan: the build bin runs about $25 to $35 per class set new (quart and gallon zip-top bags, hair gel or clear dish soap, food coloring, large pom-poms and buttons, paper plates, paper cups, construction paper, yarn, painter's tape, packing tape, glue sticks, large craft sticks, index cards). Most of it lasts across periods and years.
- The $5 limit is priced from the posted bin price list, not from a store, so the arithmetic is the same for every student and the limit is about choosing, not shopping.
- The tube rule is the safety line that matters most. Say it on all three days. A kit for a 2 or 3 year old with a loose small piece is not done.
- Donating the kits: a pre-K or an elementary after-school program in the district, a library children's room, or a family shelter's play area, with permission and a second safety look by that adult. Only kits that passed every checklist line, and never with a student's last name on the card.

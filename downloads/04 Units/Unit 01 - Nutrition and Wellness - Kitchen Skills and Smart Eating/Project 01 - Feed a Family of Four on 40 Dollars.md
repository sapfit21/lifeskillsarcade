## The scenario

A family of four has $40 for tonight's dinner. That is $10 a person. They want to eat well. They have a few rules (your family card tells you what they are), a pantry with a few things already in it, and about 45 minutes to cook.

Your team is the family's meal planner. You will plan the dinner, price it to the penny from this week's store circular, check it against MyPlate, cook one dish from it in our kitchen, make a menu card, and pitch it to the class in sixty seconds. The class votes on which team's dinner best fits its family and its budget.

## The two budgets

- **$40** is the family's dinner. Four full servings, everything on the plate, priced from the circular. You pay for the whole package, not the spoonful you use. Pantry items on your family card cost $0.
- **$20** is the lab budget. That is what I can spend so your team can cook one dish from your plan for seven tasting portions in our kitchen. Your station card has to come in at $20 or under.

## The room's rules for the cooked dish

- No knives. Anything that needs cutting comes pre-cut, pre-shredded, or gets torn or shredded with two forks.
- Pre-cooked meat only (deli rotisserie chicken, frozen cooked meatballs, canned beans if no meat). You reheat it to 165 F. You do not cook raw meat.
- Stovetop and oven only. No frying.
- I handle the oven door and I drain the pasta. You call me.
- Twenty minutes of cooking time. If it takes longer, it is the wrong dish for the lab (it can still be on your $40 plan).

## Team roles

Planning roles (Lessons 1.18, 1.19, 1.21). Every team has the four core roles. Teams of five to seven add the expansion roles in order.

| Role | Your job |
|---|---|
| Planner (core) | Runs the plan sheet. Reads the family card. Makes sure every dish has a group and the plate has four. |
| Shopper (core) | Owns the circular. Finds every price, size, and unit. Says "you pay for the whole package." |
| Nutrition Checker (core) | Runs the MyPlate check. Checks every dish against every rule on the family card. Writes the nutrition claim. |
| Presenter (core) | Owns the menu card and the pitch. Reports the totals. Stays at the station during the gallery walk. |
| Second Shopper (5th) | Prices the sides and the drink. Double checks the Shopper's units. |
| Recorder (6th) | Keeps the clean team copy. Writes the revision log. |
| Materials Manager (7th) | Runs the calculators and the running total. Keeps the circulars, cards, and pens in one place. |

Lab roles (Lesson 1.20 Day 2): Station Manager, Prep Lead, Cook Lead, Plate Captain, Clean Crew (two), Safety and Sanitation Checker. You choose these on Day 1 of the lab and write them on the station card. Nobody has to hold the same job in the plan and the lab.

## Where you get to choose

- **The family card.** Three families, three sets of rules. Your team gets one. Within the card, the dinner is yours.
- **The dishes.** Anything the family can eat, the circular sells, and $40 covers.
- **The station dish.** Any dish from your plan that fits the room's rules and $20. If yours cannot work, I have three fallback cards (a rice and bean bowl, pasta with vegetables and jarred sauce, chicken quesadillas) and you pick the closest one.
- **The pitch.** Spoken, sung, a recorded sixty-second video, a read script, or a drawn menu poster with a teammate explaining it. A jingle is welcome. The claim has to be true.

The project counts in the labs and projects category (40 percent of the marking period). A late project is accepted with a one-level drop on one criterion, never a zero.

# Teacher notes on the project

- This is Sal's Feed a Family of Four on $40 from Brentwood and SEYS 536, spread across a 40-minute schedule with the checkpoints he said it needed. His original had the $12 vs $38 hook, the unit price and MyPlate mini lesson, the ingredient relay, team planning with flyers and the $40 cap, a peer math check, a pitch and jingle, a class vote, and an exit reflection. All of those are here except the ingredient relay, which is a MyPlate activity and belongs in Topic 1.3 (Lesson 1.10) rather than the project launch.
- Three families, three teams per class, one family per team. Two classes planning for the same family is fine; two teams in one class is not, because the vote becomes a taste contest.
- The $40 plan is for four full servings at home; the $20 station card is seven tasting portions of one dish. They are two different numbers on purpose. Students who confuse them are told: the $40 is the family's money; the $20 is mine.
- Budget for the unit plan: $20 per station, three stations per period, times the number of periods, plus about $8 per team for the fresh tasting item in Lesson 1.21 if leftovers cannot be used. Fallback dishes cost $11.05 to $17.64 per station.
- Prices in the handouts are realistic Long Island supermarket prices for September 2026 and every one is to be refreshed from the current circular before teaching. The unit price math still works if the prices change; only the answers in the teacher keys move.

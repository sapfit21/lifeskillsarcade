# Lesson 0.4: Teamwork Under Pressure

**Unit:** Unit 0, Welcome to the FACS Room (Launch; no content module)
**Topic:** Day 4 of the five-day launch
**Grade:** 6 to 8 (written at grade 7; see Differentiation for the grade 6 support and grade 8 stretch)
**Days:** 1 class period of 40 minutes
**Room:** Either (a table with water and rice is all it needs; the FACS lab is better because the stations become the teams' home base)

## Enduring understanding and essential question

**Big idea:** A team that cannot talk still has to get the job done, and the way it manages that shows what the team is made of. Good teams write down how they will treat each other before the pressure starts, not after.
**Essential question:** What does your team do when it cannot talk, and what does that tell you about what it needs when it can?

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. (Low) Given a role card, state their own role's job during the silent challenge and identify who on the team holds each of the four roles.
2. (Mid) Measure four amounts of water and rice as a team in silence, then analyze what the team did without words (pointing, showing, waiting, taking over) and which of those helped or hurt.
3. (High) Write a team norms contract of four norms, each stated as a behavior someone could see, agreed to by every member, and signed.

## Vocabulary

Tier 2 (general academic): norm, behavior, contract, pressure
Tier 3 (FACS): role card, Station Manager, level off, liquid measure, dry measure, team norms. See the unit vocabulary list for Turkish, Portuguese, and Spanish.

## Materials and setup

- Per team of four: a dry measuring cup set, a liquid measuring cup, a set of measuring spoons, a bowl of uncooked white rice (about 2 cups), a pitcher of water, four paper cups labeled 1 to 4, a tray to catch spills, a paper towel, and a team card with the four targets (from `Handout 00.04 - Team Norms Contract.md`, Part 1)
- Four role cards per team (Station Manager, Head Cook, Second Cook, Prep Cook) from `Handout 01.08 - Role Cards Lab 1.md`, with the day 4 note on the handout that says what each does today
- A check station at the front: one dry cup set, one liquid cup, one spoon set, and, if the room has it, the gram scale (optional; the eye check works)
- Handout, one per student: `Handout 00.04 - Team Norms Contract.md`
- Slides: `Slides 00.04 - Teamwork Under Pressure`
- Timer; the reset cue
- Setup notes: no ingredients other than rice and water; no heat; no knives. Rice spills sweep up; put the rice bowls on the trays. The same rice bag is reused in Unit 1 Lesson 1.6. Allergy check: rice and water only; no allergy risk. Write the do now on the board.

## Pre-assessment

Yesterday's clipboard note on who spoke and who did not at each table. Read it before class; today's silence removes talking as an advantage, and the students who did not speak yesterday often lead today. The do now asks what a team needs from each member; the answers are the raw material for the norms.

## Do now and hook (Time: 4 min)

On the board: "Yesterday your team ranked ten items. Finish this sentence: 'The one thing my team needed more of yesterday was ___.' One line. Then: 'The one thing I did well for my team was ___.'"

Debrief: four first lines on the board (usually: listening, letting everyone talk, staying on task, not arguing). Leave them up; they come back in Step 4. Then the hook: "In a real lab, four of you share one station, one stove, and twelve minutes. Today you get water, rice, and four measurements. And nobody may say a word."

## Procedure

| Step | Teacher will | Students will | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Direct instruction: the four roles today | Hand each team the four role cards and point to the day 4 box on the handout: Station Manager reads the team card and points to who does what and does the final check; Head Cook measures items 1 and 3; Second Cook measures items 2 and 4; Prep Cook fills the cups, holds the tray, wipes spills. Show the two techniques on one slide: dry (spoon rice in, level off with a straight edge); liquid (pour, set the cup on the table, read at eye level). Say the rules: no talking, no writing, no mouthing words; pointing and showing are allowed; the reset cue ends it. | Take a role card. Read the day 4 box. Watch the two techniques. | 4 min |
| 2. Learning activity: the Silent Measurement preview | Start the timer. Say nothing except the time calls at 5 and 8 minutes. Watch for: the person who takes over, the person who waits to be told, the pointing that works, the team that stops to show each other the technique. Note one thing per team. At 10 minutes, use the reset cue. Then walk the trays to the check station and check each cup by pouring it into the matching measure (or weighing it): on the line, close, or off. | In silence: the Station Manager assigns by pointing; the cooks measure the four targets into cups 1 to 4 (for example, 1 cup water; 1/2 cup rice, leveled; 2 tablespoons rice; 1/4 cup water); the Prep Cook fills and wipes. Carry the tray to the check station when done and stand behind it. | 10 min |
| 3. Debrief: what worked without words | Say the results for each team (four on, three on, and so on). Then the debrief question: "What did your team do without words? What helped? What hurt?" Give the four choices on the slide: pointing, showing, waiting, taking over. Share the one thing you noted per team, without names. | Talk (finally). Each team fills Part 2 of the handout: what we did without words, what helped, what hurt, and one thing we would do differently. | 5 min |
| 4. Learning activity: write the team norms | Show the norms rule: a norm is a behavior someone could see, not a feeling. "Be nice" is a feeling. "Ask the quiet person before we decide" is a norm. Show the four do now lines from the board and three examples from the slide (we wait for the Station Manager's point before we start; we say the amount out loud before we pour; we clean our own spill; nobody leaves until the station check). Teams write four norms and every member signs. | Write four norms in Part 3. Test each one aloud: "Could Mr. Prevosto see us doing this?" If not, rewrite it. All four sign. | 12 min |

## Questions to ask

Monitor understanding:
- What does the Station Manager do that nobody else does today?
- Cup 2 was half a cup of rice. What is the one technique word for getting it right?
- Which of the four (pointing, showing, waiting, taking over) did your team use most?

Deepen learning:
- Somebody at your table took over. Did the team measure better or worse because of it? How do you know?
- What would you write on a norm to fix the thing your team needed more of yesterday?
- In a real lab, what happens to the team norms at minute 11 when the eggs are burning? What norm would survive that?

## Check for understanding

Two checks. During Step 2, the clipboard note per team: "Station Manager assigns before anyone measures," "nobody talks," "someone shows a technique instead of grabbing." During Step 4, walk and read the norms; a norm that is a feeling ("respect each other") gets the question "what would I see?" and a rewrite. A "got it" contract has four norms that all name a visible behavior.

## Closure (Time: 5 min)

Each team reads one norm aloud. Write the eight or so norms on chart paper and leave it up; tomorrow's Room and Lab Agreement pulls from it. Exit card, two lines: my role today and one thing I did in it; the norm from another team I wish we had written. Collect the signed team norms contracts. Sort exit cards: "got it" names a role and an action; "needs more" names a role only, and those students get a role card reread at the start of Lab 1.

## Differentiation and supports

- ELL: the challenge is silent by design, so language is not the barrier; the two techniques are on a picture slide; the team card has pictures of the four targets next to the words; the norm examples are in Turkish, Portuguese, and Spanish on the handout (see `Vocabulary 00.md`); the norm sentence starter is "We ___ before we ___."
- IEP and 504 (general): a student who cannot measure holds the Prep Cook role (fills, wipes, carries the tray) and signs the norms; a student who finds silence stressful may be the Station Manager, who points and does not measure; the norms may be dictated to the recorder; extra time by writing norm four as tomorrow's do now.
- Grade 6 support: three targets instead of four (drop the 2 tablespoons); three norms.
- Grade 8 stretch: a fifth target, 1 teaspoon rice, with the spoon leveled; the team writes a fifth norm that says what the team does when someone breaks a norm; the student writes two sentences comparing this team to the survival team yesterday.
- UDL checkpoint used: multiple means of engagement (a timed team challenge with a check that is not a grade) and multiple means of action and expression (a student contributes by pointing, showing, measuring, wiping, or writing).
- No-kitchen alternative: this lesson needs only a table, water, rice, and cups. It runs in any classroom; put the trays on desks pushed together.

## Teacher notes

- If the period runs short, cut Step 3 to two minutes (results and one observation) and protect Step 4. The norms are tomorrow's raw material.
- Common mistake: a team talks in the first thirty seconds. Do not stop the clock; note it, and use it in the debrief ("one team broke the silence at second twenty; what did that cost them?").
- Common mistake two: the norms are feelings. Every team writes "respect" first. The test question ("what would I see?") fixes it. Ask it at every table.
- The check station is not a grade. Say that before the timer starts, or the challenge turns into a test and the quiet students freeze.
- The rice goes back in the bag and comes out again on day 13 for Lesson 1.6. Label the bag.

## How to use this rubric

- **Hand it out twice.** Criteria 1 and 2 in Lesson 7.5 at the start of Step 4, so students read the descriptors before the interview is scored. The whole rubric in Lesson 7.7, at the project launch, so teams plan against criteria 3 to 6 from the first day. Nothing on this rubric should be a surprise to anybody on any day.
- **Score criteria 1 and 2 inside the last 15 minutes of Lesson 7.5,** from the stapled Topic 7.1 set (resume, three-part answer, STAR planner, peer feedback sheet) plus the teacher's two live marks from the interview rounds: did the three-part answer have all three parts, and did the STAR story have a Result. **Return criteria 1 and 2 at the start of Lesson 7.7** so every student knows where they stand before the project takes over the unit.
- **Score criteria 3, 4, and 5 from the folder and the object at the end of Lesson 7.10 Day 2,** not from the poster on pitch day. The folder is the evidence. **Return them at the door on pitch day,** before teams pitch. A team that knows it earned a 4 on its numbers pitches differently.
- **Score criterion 6 live in Lesson 7.11,** on a clipboard, in the twenty-second swap between teams and never during a pitch. Mark four things only: did all three speak, did it come in at or under 60 seconds, was the money said out loud, did somebody answer a judge question with a number or a quote. Fill the rest of the descriptor from the judge sheets after class.
- **The judge sheets are not the grade and judges are told so.** They are evidence that feeds criterion 6 and they are read before the score is finalized. A judge's low score on a team the teacher watched succeed does not lower the grade; a judge's specific written comment about a team's numbers may raise it.
- **The self-assessment row is filled first, always.** A team whose self-scores land within one point of the teacher's on every criterion earns the Exceeds level on criterion 3 if the rest of that row is met. Honest self-assessment is part of the work and it is the only place on this rubric where the student's own judgment moves the score.
- **Every descriptor is written as what the student or team actually did.** If work does not match any box, pick the closest and write why on the notes line.
- **Accommodations do not cap any score.** A resume that was dictated, a pitch part written and read aloud by a teammate, a pitch delivered to the panel with no audience, a reflection written in a home language, a picture-panel deck, a drawn prototype: all of these can score at 4. Criterion 6's Meets level explicitly says "or a filed accommodation covers a member's part being written and read by a teammate," and the Exceeds level's "without reading it start to finish" is not applied to a student whose accommodation is to read. Write the accommodation on the notes line so the score is defensible later.
- **The one individual exception on the team criteria.** If one student did not do their role's work and the team can name what was not done, that student is scored one level lower on the criterion they owned (CEO criterion 3, CFO criterion 4, CMO criterion 5) and the rest of the team is not penalized. Use this rarely and only with evidence from the planner sheets and the teacher's own observation, never from one teammate's complaint.
- **A student absent on pitch day** is scored on criteria 3, 4, and 5 with the team and on criterion 6 from their written pitch part, per the course grading plan. Nobody loses project points for being sick.
- **A late folder** is accepted with a one-level drop on one criterion, never a zero.
- **Where this score goes.** All 24 points go in the Labs and projects category (40 percent). The Lesson 7.12 year reflection is scored separately and, with this rubric, serves as the course final; there is no unit test for Unit 7 and no final exam.
- **Return the completed rubric with the portfolio in Lesson 7.12,** and have students copy the six numbers onto the portfolio checklist before the folder goes home. The rubric goes home in the folder. Photograph it first, along with the resume and the reflection, for the program's own records.

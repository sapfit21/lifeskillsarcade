## 1. NYS Middle Level CTE framework for FACS (nyctecenter.org and nysed.gov)

### 1.1 The module pages and what they say

Main modules page: https://nyctecenter.org/mlmodules

Verbatim text from that page:

- "Theme modules are the basis for middle-level CTE. They are foundational, cross-content, and standards-based."
- "Content modules supply context for the Themes. They are specific to each CTE content area, standards-based, and connect middle-level to high school."

Overview page: https://nyctecenter.org/instruction/middle-level-cte

Verbatim text from that page:

- "In September 2017, the Board of Regents made a permanent revision to CR 100.4 (c) as it pertains to CTE. The regulation provides that all middle-level students are entitled to 1 ¾ unit career and technical education (CTE)."
- The framework "comprises 37 instructional modules": six Theme Modules and 31 Content Modules (the six FACS content modules are among the 31).
- Schools design "project-based learning experiences that will help our middle-school students gain foundational CTE knowledge and skills through the lens of the CTE content area(s)" available in the district.

Learning experiences page: https://nyctecenter.org/ml-learning

Verbatim text: the middle school CTE curriculum consists of "37 modules: Six Theme Modules, which are the basis for middle school CTE; and, 31 Content Modules." Each sample learning experience illustrates "how CTE Content provides the context through which middle school learners develop the transferable skills represented by the Themes."

That sentence is the clearest statement on the site of how the two module types combine: a Theme Module names the transferable skill (the "what students take away"), and a Content Module supplies the subject-area context (the "through what"). A learning experience is written as one Theme plus one Content Module. Nothing on nyctecenter.org says a program must cover a set number of modules or all six themes.

### 1.5 The regulation: Commissioner's Regulation 100.4(c) and the "Home and Career Skills" question

NYSED middle level CTE requirement page: https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/new-york-state-middle-level-cte-requirement

Verbatim: "In September 2017, the Board of Regents revised the CTE portion of Commissioner's Regulations 100.4(c)" so that "all middle-level students are entitled to 1 ¾ unit career and technical education, instruction can begin as early as grade 5, and instruction can be delivered by teachers certified in any title in any CTE content area, so long as it is through the lens of their content area."

Regulation text (NYSED Office of Counsel, Part 100 full text index): https://www.counsel.nysed.gov/rules/indices-fulltext/2023/05

Verbatim from 100.4(c)(1) as amended: students "shall be provided instruction designed to enable them to achieve, by the end of grade 8, the applicable State learning standards as prescribed in subdivision (t) of section 100.1 of this Part through:" ... "career and technical education (i.e., agricultural education, business and marketing education, computer science education, family and consumer sciences education, health sciences education, technology education, and/or trade and technical education), for a total of one and three-fourths units of study" ... "health education, one half unit of study, as required by section 135.3(c) of this Title" ... "the arts, including one half unit of study in the visual arts, and one half unit of study in music". The struck prior language reads "[home and career skills, three quarters of a unit of study]" and "[technology education, one unit of study]".

So: the 3/4 unit Home and Career Skills requirement no longer exists as a separate line in 100.4. It was folded into the 1 3/4 unit CTE requirement in 2017. A school that keeps 30 weeks of FACS and 40 weeks of technology education still meets the requirement (see FAQ below), but a charter school can meet the 1 3/4 units in other combinations.

NYSED middle level requirements page (section 100.4 summary): https://www.nysed.gov/curriculum-instruction/middle-level-education-program-requirements
Verbatim: "Career and Technical Education, one and three quarters unit"; "Health education, one half unit of study as required by section 135.3(c)"; "Units of study, not units of credit, are applicable to the middle grades (specifically, grades 7 and 8)."; unit of study requirements for the middle grades are to be met "by the end of grade 8."

NYSED Middle Level CTE Requirement FAQ: https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/new-york-state-middle-level-cte-requirement-faq

Verbatim Q and A most relevant to this build:

- "What grades are covered by the middle level CTE requirement?" "The middle level requirement applies to Grades 5-8. Instruction may begin as early as Grade 5 as long as it is taught by a certified career and technical education teacher who holds certification to teach Grades 5 and 6."
- "Is the Home and Career Skills curriculum still in use for the middle level requirement?" "With the revision of the middle level requirement, six new family and consumer sciences content modules were developed to address the six-overarching middle level CTE themes. As such, the Home and Career Skills curriculum is no longer being updated."
- "Our school is happy having 40 weeks of technology education and 30 weeks of family and consumer sciences education? Do we need to change anything?" "No. 40 weeks (1 unit) of technology education and 30 weeks (¾ unit) of family and consumer sciences meets the middle level (1 ¾ unit) requirement."
- "Are there a minimum number of different CTE instructional areas that middle school students need to be exposed to?" "No. Schools decide which CTE instructional areas their students will be exposed to in meeting the 1 ¾ unit requirement."
- "Can we provide students 'elective' choices to meet the 1 ¾ unit requirement or does instruction need to be the same for all students in our school/district?" "Students may be provided with different options to meet the 1 ¾ unit middle level requirement provided that all student groups have equal access."
- "What teachers can teach the middle level requirement?" "Any teacher who is defined as a career and technical education teacher in Section 100.1(l) of Commissioner's Regulations may teach the middle level requirement. These are agriculture, business, computer science, family and consumer sciences, health sciences, technology, and trade/technical teachers."
- "Can a CTE teacher teach content outside of their CTE area?" "No. CTE teachers may only teach the middle level content that aligns with the subject area of their teacher certification(s)."
- "Can the 20-week middle school health course count toward the 1 ¾ unit CTE requirement?" "No. The middle school health education requirement as defined in section 135.3(b) of Commissioner's Regulations is separate from the 1 ¾ unit CTE requirement."
- "Can high school CTE courses be used to meet the 1 ¾ unit middle level requirement?" "Yes. They may be used to meet the 1 ¾ unit middle level requirement in accordance with Section 100.4(d) of Commissioner's Regulations."
- "What is the middle level CTE curriculum?" The answer names the six theme modules: Career and Community Opportunities, Communication and Interpersonal Relationships, Financial and Consumer Literacy, Health Safety and Wellness, Problem Solving and Innovation, and Sustainability.
- "How should middle school CTE course data be reported?" Schools report using "SCED codes to report course-level data" by grade level and CTE content area.

The FAQ does not say that all six themes or all six FACS content modules must be taught, does not define a unit of study in minutes, and does not address charter schools.

Naming: NYSED and nyctecenter.org now say "family and consumer sciences" (FACS) throughout. "Home and Career Skills" (HCS) is the name of the 2005 core curriculum and of the pre-2017 3/4 unit requirement. Since the FAQ says the HCS curriculum "is no longer being updated," the curriculum for this build should be titled Family and Consumer Sciences and cite the modules, with a one-line note that it satisfies what districts used to call Home and Career Skills.

NYSED FACS landing page: https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/family-and-consumer-sciences (FACS Associate listed: Kelli Grab, Kelli.Grab@nysed.gov). Verbatim description: FACS is a CTE content area in which students "explore self, family, and community to meet present and future responsibilities as family and community members."

NYSED FACS Learning Standards page: https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/facs-learning-standards. It links three standards sources: the NYS Learning Standards PDF (section 2 below), the National FACS standards at http://www.leadfcsed.org/national-standards.html, and the Common Career Technical Core at https://www.careertech.org/cctc. It says the department worked with educators to develop "theme modules and content modules" for the middle level and points to the middle level CTE page for sample projects.

### Status of newer NYS FACS standards and the Home and Career Skills core curriculum

- There are no newer NYS FACS learning standards. The 1996 Health, PE, and FACS standards remain the ones NYSED links from its FACS Learning Standards page (https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/facs-learning-standards). NYLearns files them under "Next Generation Standards" only as a site category; the content is the 1996 text.
- Home and Career Skills Core Curriculum: "New York State Family and Consumer Sciences Education: Home and Career Skills Core Curriculum," The University of the State of New York, The State Education Department, September 2005, grades 5 to 8. It integrates four process skills (Communication, Leadership, Management, Thinking) with ten content topics (Career Development; Clothing Management; Community Connections; Consumer Resource Management; Family/Parenting; Financial Management; Human Development; Interpersonal Relationships; Nutrition and Wellness; Personal Environment Management). It states: "Part 100.4 of the Commissioner's Regulations states that every student must complete a 3/4 unit course in Home and Career Skills by the end of grade 8." Copy fetched from NYSUT: https://www.nysut.org/~/media/Files/NYSUT/Resources/1900/January/HE_CORE.pdf. NYSED says this curriculum "is no longer being updated" (FAQ, section 1.5). It is still a useful crosswalk because its ten topics map almost one to one onto the six 2018 content modules.
- The old p12.nysed.gov/cte/facse address now redirects into www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/family-and-consumer-sciences. Could not verify the archived old page (Wayback blocked).
- NYSED still hosts high school FACS syllabi that show the commencement end of the sequence, for example "Food and Nutrition Core" (https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/career-technical-education/food-and-nutrition-core.pdf) and "Food Preparation and Nutrition" (https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/career-technical-education/food-preparation-and-nutrition.pdf). ### Standard 3a: Universal Foundation Skills

Standard statement (CDOS.I.3a): "Students will demonstrate mastery of the foundation skills and competencies essential for success in the workplace."
Source: https://nylearns.org/module/standards/Tools/Browse?standardId=185449

Key ideas (each with its one Intermediate performance indicator). Key idea text from NYLearns; performance indicator text from the Xello reproduction of the NYSED document, with the Basic Skills and Managing Information indicators also confirmed on NYLearns (https://nylearns.org/module/standards/Tools/Browse?standardId=185498 and https://nylearns.org/module/standards/Tools/Browse?standardId=185503).

- Basic Skills (CDOS.I.3a.BS.1): "Basic skills include the ability to read, write, listen, and speak as well as perform arithmetical and mathematical functions."
  Intermediate PI: "Students listen to and read the ideas of others and analyze what they hear and read; acquire and use information from a variety of sources; and apply a combination of mathematical operations to solve problems in oral or written form."
- Thinking Skills (CDOS.I.3a.TS.2): "Thinking skills lead to problem solving, experimenting, and focused observation and allow the application of knowledge to new and unfamiliar situations."
  Intermediate PI: "Students evaluate facts, solve advanced problems, and make decisions by applying logic and reasoning skills."
- Personal Qualities (CDOS.I.3a.PQ.3): "Personal qualities generally include competence in self-management and the ability to plan, organize, and take independent action."
  Intermediate PI: "Students demonstrate an understanding of the relationship between individuals and society and interact with others in a positive manner."
- Interpersonal Skills (CDOS.I.3a.IS.4): "Positive interpersonal qualities lead to teamwork and cooperation in large and small groups in family, social, and work situations."
  Intermediate PI: "Students demonstrate the ability to work with others, present facts that support arguments, listen to dissenting points of view, and reach a shared decision."
- Technology (CDOS.I.3a.TE.5): "Technology is the process and product of human skill and ingenuity in designing and creating things from available resources to satisfy personal and societal needs and wants."
  Intermediate PI: "Students select and use appropriate technology to complete a task."

## 6. NYS Health Education requirements that overlap nutrition (for coordination with the health teacher)

Regulation: Commissioner's Regulation 100.4(c)(1) requires "health education, one half unit of study, as required by section 135.3(c)" for grades 7 and 8. Source: https://www.counsel.nysed.gov/rules/indices-fulltext/2023/05 and https://www.nysed.gov/curriculum-instruction/middle-level-education-program-requirements. The NYSED FAQ confirms the health course and the CTE requirement are separate and the 20-week health course cannot count toward the 1 3/4 CTE units: https://www.nysed.gov/career-technical-education/new-york-state-middle-level-cte-requirement-faq

CR 135.3 (as summarized in the NYS Center for School Health "New York State School Health Education Requirements Summary," 4/27/21): for grades 7 to 8, "provision shall also be made for a separate one-half-year course" in health education "taught by teachers holding a certificate to teach health." Required middle school topics called out in that summary: HIV/AIDS, mental health, alcohol and other drugs, and child sexual abuse prevention (recommended to be embedded). The summary does not list nutrition as a separately mandated topic in 135.3. Source: https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1736338911/schoolhealthservicesnycom/ohdjrtyoglttqg57a8md/nysschoolhealtheducationreqsummary.pdf (redirect target of https://www.schoolhealthny.com/cms/lib/NY01832015/Centricity/Domain/159/NYSSchoolHealthEducationReqSummary.pdf)

Where nutrition sits in health education: the same 1996 standards document covers both FACS and Health Education. Standard 1 is shared in title ("Personal Health and Fitness") but the two strands have different key ideas. The Health Education strand at Intermediate reads (NYLearns, https://nylearns.org/module/standards/Tools/Browse?standardId=185393):
- Key Idea HPF.HE.I.1.1: "Students will understand human growth and development and recognize the relationship between behaviors and healthy development."
- HPF.HE.I.1.1.A: "Students integrate knowledge of basic body systems with an understanding of the changes that accompany puberty."
- HPF.HE.I.1.1.B: "Students apply prevention and risk reduction strategies to adolescent health problems."
- HPF.HE.I.1.1.C: "Students demonstrate the necessary knowledge and skills to promote healthy adolescent development."
- HPF.HE.I.1.1.D: "Students analyze the multiple influences which affect health decisions and behaviors."

The FACS strand of Standard 1 (section 2 above) is the one that names nutrition, food safety, and meal preparation. So under the state standards, nutrition content is assigned to FACS at least as much as to health, and the FACS course is the one expected to cover food selection, meal preparation, and food safety. The overlap to coordinate is: the health class typically covers dietary guidelines, body image, eating disorders, and physical activity as part of "adolescent health problems" and "influences on health decisions"; the FACS class covers reading labels, planning and shopping for meals, cooking, and food safety.

NYSED's "Guidance Document for Achieving the New York State Standards in Health Education" (the department's health curriculum guidance; it lists functional knowledge areas, one of which is physical activity and nutrition): https://p12.nysed.gov/sss/documents/GuidanceDocument4.25.update.pdf. Could not fetch (site blocked to automated tools); verify the nutrition area wording by hand.

Practical flag for the curriculum: in the Nutrition and Wellness unit, add a "Coordinate with health" note at the lessons on dietary guidelines (MyPlate), eating disorders and fad diets (National FCS 14.2.3), and body image, and point out that the FACS labs, label reading, shopping, and meal planning lessons are not duplicated in health.

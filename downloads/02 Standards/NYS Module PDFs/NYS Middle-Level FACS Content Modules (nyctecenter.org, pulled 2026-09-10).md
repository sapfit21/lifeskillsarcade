# NYS Middle-Level CTE: the six FACS Content Modules

Source: NYS CTE Technical Assistance Center, https://nyctecenter.org/mlmodules (the "Middle-Level CTE" site the syllabus names and Prof. Barnaby clicked through in class Sep 10). Each module is a PDF at https://nyctecenter.org/images/files/Middle_Level_CTE/Content_Modules_/0N<Name>.pdf. Text below was extracted from the PDFs on 2026-09-10 via a fetch tool; wording is close to the original but check the PDF before quoting a line in graded work. Competency numbers for the National FCS Standards are as printed in each PDF.

Why this file exists: question 1 of her Concept Map ("What content is included in the NYS Curriculum Guides for this unit?") is answered from these pages. She also wants every unit to hit at least three of the six CTE theme modules (Career and Community Opportunities; Communication and Interpersonal Relationships; Financial and Consumer Literacy; Health, Safety, and Wellness; Problem Solving and Innovation; Sustainability). Each module PDF already has one illustrative activity per theme, listed below, which is the fastest way to answer question 2.

Middle-level CTE requirement (same site): all middle-level students are entitled to 1 3/4 units of CTE; instruction can begin as early as grade 5 and can be delivered in any CTE content area. The three NYS FACS learning standards at the intermediate level: 1 Personal Health and Fitness; 2 Safe and Healthy Environment; 3 Resource Management.

## 1. Individual Growth and Life Readiness (IGLR)
Her narrowed topics (FACS Topics sheet): personal values, employability skills, ways to save money, credit vs debit, budgeting.

Description: how self-understanding influences lifelong decision-making. Students explore, identify, and assess personal traits, interests, and abilities for contributing to home, school, community, and work. Covers basic money management and career exploration.
Guiding question: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate introductory understanding of self as it relates to personal development, employability, and financial readiness?

Content strands (students will):
- Personal Development: examine personal values; discover and evaluate personality traits and strengths; predict areas of personal interest relating to the future.
- Goal Setting: role of values and standards in goal setting; differentiate SMART and stretch goals; set a goal for personal improvement; design, implement, and evaluate a plan to reach it.
- Employability: explore employability skills; interpersonal dynamics; effective communication; strategies for career readiness.
- Financial Readiness: identify sources of income; create and follow a budget; options for money transfers; balance a bank account; ways to save; compare types of credit and loans; impact of family transitions on personal finance.
- Career Pathways: explore careers within the 16 national career clusters; skills needed to be an entrepreneur.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (values, strengths, personality, and job-skills inventories feeding resume, references, cover letter, interview practice); Communication (interview an adult in a field of interest, with role-played prep); Financial (short-term savings goal, compare savings products, justify the choice); Health/Safety/Wellness (3-D personal timeline: where I have been, am, and am going); Problem Solving (entrepreneur traits, small-group business plan and prototype, pitch to a panel); Sustainability (resources used by leisure activities and how to preserve them).

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a (intermediate); NYS FACS 2 and 3; Career Ready Practices 1, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11; National FCS 1.0 (1.2.1-1.2.4, 1.2.8), 2.0 (2.1.1-2.1.2, 2.1.7, 2.4.3, 2.5.1, 2.5.3, 2.6.1-2.6.2, 2.7.2-2.7.4), 12.0 (12.1.1, 12.2.4), 13.0 (13.1.1, 13.2.1, 13.3.1-13.3.3).

Note: her worked example (Week 02 IGLR files) uses this module: Personal Development 7 to 8 days, Career Pathways 12 to 13 days, Career Box project.

## 2. Human Development and Relationships (HDR)
Her narrowed topics: peer pressure, managing stress, healthy vs unhealthy relationships, family influence on adolescent development, types of families, roles and responsibilities of parents.

Description: social, emotional, physical, and intellectual stages of development; building constructive relationships within family contexts; what makes a healthy family; parenting responsibilities; family diversity and support systems; recognizing personal capacities; decision-making and managing challenges; careers in human development and human services.
Guiding questions: how do positive individual and family relationships across the lifespan help human beings develop and thrive? What impact does the family have on the well-being of individuals and society?

Content strands (students will):
- Human Growth and Development Across the Lifespan: developmental stages; links between physical, emotional, social, and intellectual development; infant and young-child care procedures; design experiences that promote child growth.
- Self-Concept and Self-Image: define self-concept and what forms it; positive vs negative self-concept; how self-concept changes; how image is projected through appearance, communication, conduct; first impressions.
- Peer Influence and Stress Management: define peer and peer pressure; compare family, peer, media, and other influences; analyze peer pressure and strategies for autonomy; common adolescent challenges; stressors and management approaches.
- Human Relationships: characteristics of healthy relationships; of unhealthy relationships; ways to strengthen relationships.
- Family Role in Society: significance of family for well-being; family as the basic social unit; family structures and transitions; cultural traditions and diversity; family influence on adolescent self-concept, personality, independence; support groups, agencies, organizations.
- Parenting: parenting roles and responsibilities and their effect on well-being; readiness factors for parenting.
- Career Pathways: human development and human services careers; assess personal fit.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (design and run developmentally appropriate activities for an elementary afterschool program); Communication (video clips of unhealthy relationship patterns, then re-enact with respect, boundaries, accountability, compromise); Financial (family budget scenario with childcare costs); Health/Safety/Wellness (mindfulness journal on stress, peer pressure, gratitude); Problem Solving (find toy hazards, prototype a safer toy, test with children); Sustainability (replicate the marshmallow delayed-gratification study, apply to long-term problems).

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a; NYS FACS 2; Career Ready Practices 3, 4, 5, 9, 12; Human Services cluster 2, 5, 6; Early Childhood pathway 1, 2, 7, 8; Family and Community Services pathway 2; National FCS 6.0 (6.1, 6.2), 15.0 (15.1, 15.2).

## 3. Apparel and Textile Production and Design
Her narrowed topics: appropriate clothing for roles and activities, sustainability practices for repurposing apparel, comparing cost of the same apparel from different sources, hand sewing equipment, basics of hand sewing.

Description: how personal circumstances, culture, and preference shape textile and apparel choices; selecting, making, caring for, and repairing clothing; clothing as expression; appropriate clothing for contexts; careers in textiles and apparel.
Guiding question: what knowledge and skills are necessary for selecting, purchasing, creating, and maintaining apparel and textiles for a variety of activities and settings?

Content strands (students will):
- Selection: personal needs, wants, values; appropriate clothing for roles and activities; image and style through clothing; wardrobe inventory; select items within resources; select for situations without exceeding financial and other resources.
- Production: common hand and machine sewing equipment and supplies; safe use and storage; basic hand and machine techniques by creating or repairing a textile product; sustainability practices for repurposing apparel.
- Maintenance: care procedures that extend wear; care labels; daily, weekly, seasonal care.
- Consumer Resources and Finance: cost of constructing vs purchasing vs repurposing; compare cost of similar items across vendor types; clothing budget; global responsibility in apparel choices.
- Career Pathways: career paths in textiles and apparel; investigate one career and its pathway.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (local agencies that provide clothing; repair, upcycle, or make items to donate); Communication (trios teach peers about sewing equipment via matching cards); Health/Safety/Wellness (athletic and protective clothing evolution, prototype an improvement); Financial (spreadsheet clothing inventory with country of manufacture, map it, trace cost); Problem Solving (upcycling design problem in designer/client pairs: t-shirt to bag, pillowcase to shopping bag); Sustainability (commercial stain removers vs home remedies, lab test, disposal costs).

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a; NYS FACS 2; Career Ready Practices 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 11, 12; National FCS 16.0 (16.1, 16.2, 16.4).

## 4. Environmental Design and Management
Her narrowed topics: ways to conserve, influence of "green" factors on a living space, elements of design (color, line, texture, shape, space; choose one).

Description: managing living spaces for safety, resource conservation, and personal expression; household budgeting, purchasing, maintenance, problem-solving as needs change; careers in environment, housing, and interior design.
Guiding question: what knowledge and skills are necessary for designing and managing living spaces to ensure safety, conserve natural resources, and achieve personal expression?

Content strands (students will):
- Healthy, Safe, Sustainable Living Spaces: family, school, work, community settings as living space; common safety hazards and how to reduce risk; safety plans for home, work, community; resource conservation methods.
- Environment and Interior Design: design shared spaces that respect privacy and group needs; floor plans for specific needs; elements and principles of design; green/environmental factors in functional design; future housing with technology and demographics.
- Consumer Resources and Finance: budget to carry out a floor plan; consumer skills for household items.
- Career Pathways: roles in environment, housing, interior design; education and opportunities; personal fit.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (research vs guest speaker as career information sources); Communication (role play hiring a contractor); Financial (rental decisions for furniture and appliances); Health/Safety/Wellness (revise a floor plan for accessibility needs); Problem Solving (tiny house design with peer critique); Sustainability (carbon footprint activity).
(The fetch tool condensed this module's activities more than the others; open the PDF for the full activity text.)

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a; NYS FACS 2 and 3; Career Ready Practices 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10; National FCS 2.0, 3.0, 11.0 (11.1, 11.2, 11.3).

## 5. Nutrition and Wellness
Her narrowed topics: common kitchen tools/equipment and their uses, safety or sanitation practices, food budget/food purchasing strategies.

Description: how food choices and eating patterns affect health; through project-based and culinary lab experiences students build foundational skills for planning, selecting, purchasing, preparing, serving, and storing nutritious foods; careers in nutrition and wellness.
Guiding question: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate introductory skills for planning, selecting, purchasing, preparing, serving, and storing nutritious foods?

Content strands (students will):
- Culinary Management: identify common kitchen tools and equipment and their uses; safety and sanitation with food and equipment; accurate measuring of dry/solid, liquid, and small quantities; prepare a simple recipe; plan a nutritious meal; store food safely; prepare and serve a simple meal; table setting; food-sharing behaviors that respect other cultures.
- Nutrition Across the Lifespan: nutrient groups, sources, and requirements across the lifespan; nutrition and physical activity in wellness; credible dietary information; psychological influences on food choice.
- Consumer Resources and Finance: create and organize a shopping list; purchasing strategies that stretch the food budget; money's effect on the ability to buy nutritious food.
- Career Pathways: careers in nutrition, wellness, culinary arts; personal fit.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (research public health nutrition careers, teach a short nutrition activity to younger students); Communication (food allergy vs intolerance PSA); Financial (comparison shopping a week of lunch menus across vendors using circulars); Health/Safety/Wellness (food log by hand or app, evaluate and improve); Problem Solving (class kitchen safety and sanitation contract, plus safety memes); Sustainability (soda-bottle composting of lab scraps).

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a; NYS FACS 1, 2, 3; Career Ready Practices 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12; National FCS 14.1 (14.1.1-14.1.5), 14.2 (14.2.1-14.2.4), 14.3 (14.3.1-14.3.3), 14.4 (14.4.1-14.4.6), 14.5 (14.5.1-14.5.4).

## 6. Food Systems and Production
Her narrowed topics: sustainable food methods, food scarcity, decisions to purchase nutritious food for a family, basic culinary skills (select one).

Description: how climate, geography, and culture influence the availability, sustainability, and quality of food; challenges to the food supply; careers in food systems; regional cuisines and industry standards.
Guiding question: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate introductory understanding of choices, availability, sustainability, purchasing, preparation, and production of food?

Content strands (students will):
- Basic Culinary Skills: apply basic culinary and management skills; kitchen tools and equipment; safety and sanitation; accurate measuring; prepare a simple recipe.
- Food Preparation: foods of the region studied; factors affecting regional food choices; prepare regional foods with regional ingredients; regional techniques; safety and sanitation.
- Current Issues Related to Food in Global Societies: factors affecting the food supply (geography, climate, economics, transportation, farming methods, energy, population density, political systems, natural disasters, food waste); laws, regulations, policies; food-related health concerns worldwide.
- Future of Food in a Global Society: components of sustainable food systems; pros and cons of buying sustainable products; quality of sustainably produced food; consequences of food scarcity and hunger; technology and the global food supply.
- Consumer Resources and Finance: manage resources to select and purchase food; decisions about safe and nutritious food for families; consumer skills; time management and organization.
- Career Pathways: roles in food industry careers; education and training; personal fit.

Illustrative activity per CTE theme: Career and Community (community food festivals and traditional foods, community members teach a celebration dish); Communication (group videos on one step of food production, farm to consumer); Financial (cost a recipe organic vs conventional, grocery vs farmers market); Health/Safety/Wellness (adapt a health department inspection form and inspect the classroom kitchen); Problem Solving (worldwide food-related health concerns and the organizations and technologies addressing them); Sustainability (classroom herb garden used in labs).

Standards: CDOS 1, 2, 3a; NYS FACS 1, 2, 3; Career Ready Practices 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12; National FCS 14.0, 14.1 (14.1.1-14.1.5), 14.3 (14.3.3, 14.3.4), 14.4 (14.4.1-14.4.3).

## The six CTE Theme Modules: guiding questions (from nyctecenter.org/mlmodules, read 2026-09-10)
Theme modules are "foundational, cross-content, and standards-based" and can be taught by a teacher certified in any CTE area; content modules "supply context for the Themes."
- Career and Community Opportunities: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate introductory understanding of the influences that societal, economic, and technological changes have on employment and the impact that employability skills, interests, and aptitudes have on individuals' career choices and postsecondary options?
- Communication and Interpersonal Relationships: what communication and interpersonal skills can enhance an individual's ability to develop caring, respectful, effective relationships within the home, school, community, and workplace?
- Financial and Consumer Literacy: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate an introductory understanding of how money can be managed and how individuals can create and achieve financial goals while managing financial challenges?
- Health, Safety, and Wellness: what knowledge and skills are needed for individuals to consistently promote and practice safe and healthy behaviors that encourage wellness in home, school, workplace, and community settings?
- Problem Solving and Innovation: what knowledge and skills are necessary to demonstrate introductory understanding of the application of problem-solving processes and the acquisition, evaluation, and application of the products of research for informed decision making?
- Sustainability: what knowledge and skills are necessary to evaluate the long-term effects of personal practices on the environment and to demonstrate introductory understanding of how to use and conserve resources to meet human needs while minimizing harm to the environment?

## Fastest way to fill her three templates for any module (group work tonight, and Sal's own three maps for Sept 17)
1. Concept Map, question 1 (NYS content): copy the strand headings and the "students will" lines you plan to keep from the module above. Cut what you will not teach.
2. Concept Map, question 2 (CTE themes): pick three themes; the illustrative activity for each theme above is a ready-made answer for "how it fits."
3. Concept Map, questions 3 to 5 (trends, what 6th/7th graders find interesting, cross-subject links): these are the group's own thinking; her IGLR example used AI and jobs, social media influencers, personality quizzes, and math/ELA/CDOS links.
4. Unit Plan Outline: module name, 2 to 4 topics with a day count each (one day = one class period), mark which topic is the Mini Unit, list three CTE themes with one line each. Shape it exactly like `Week 02 - Example - Individual Growth and Life Unit Plan.docx`.
5. Unit Calendar: drop the topics into the Mon-Fri grid, one cell per day, end each topic with its formal assessment or project. Shape it like `Week 02 - Example - IGLR Unit Calendar Plan.docx`.

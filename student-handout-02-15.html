The rule in two lines. More buyers than sellers: the price goes up. More sellers than buyers: the price goes down. News changes how many people want to buy or sell.

Four kinds of news you have already heard. A bank fails and the borrowing stops. People still need light. One company controls almost all of a thing. A new invention arrives. The bank ignores the news.

Part A (Day 1): Match the news to the price change

Eight news cards on the left. Each one is a piece of a dispatch from 1873 or 1882, the two rounds you have already lived through. Eight price changes on the right, out of order, copied from the board. Write the letter of the price change next to the news card that caused it. Then write the cause and effect sentence for each one using this frame:

"___ happened, so more people wanted to ___ (buy / sell) ___, so the price went ___ (up / down)."

Part B (Day 2): Fact or hype?

The three tests. Score each headline: one point for each test it passes.

  • Who says it? A named company, a court, Congress, a dated report passes. "Insiders," "everyone," or no source fails.
  • Is there a number? A date, a percent, an amount, or a count passes. No number fails.
  • Does it tell you to hurry? No rush words passes. "Now," "last chance," "before it is too late," "everyone is selling" fails.

Two or three points: probably a fact. Zero or one: hype.

The year is 1893. Three of these headlines are pieces of the dispatch you heard yesterday. Three were written to make you hurry.

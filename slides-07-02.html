Slide outline for Lesson 7.2. Ten slides, one day. Slides 4, 5, and 8 stay up during the drafting block; the room needs the structure visible for fourteen minutes while the teacher is in conferences and cannot answer questions.

Slide 1

Build Your Resume

  • Unit 7, Lesson 7.2, Topic 7.1 Present Yourself
  • Today you hand me a real one
  • Not a practice one

Teacher notes

Image: a single sheet of paper and a pencil on a plain desk.

Mining sheets go back at the door. Folders are on the desks. Pencils, not pens; this is a draft and it will get crossed out.

Slide 2

Do now

  • This is a resume for a made-up eighth grader
  • Everything on it came from this class
  • Find ONE line you could write today. Write it down

Teacher notes

Image: the worked example resume from Handout 7.2 shown whole, small, so the shape is visible and the text is not readable from a distance.

Take three answers in 90 seconds. Then the hook: "You are handing me a real one by the end of this period. One you could give an adult this summer."

Slide 3

Dev Okonkwo is not real

  • Every line on this resume came from something this class actually does
  • Check it against your own folder
  • You have the same evidence

Teacher notes

Say this plainly if a student says it is fake. Yes, the person is invented; the evidence is not.

Slide 4

What this resume does that first drafts do not

  • One page. No paragraphs
  • Every bullet has a number, or a named group of people, or both
  • Experience leads with school and family work
  • It never says "hard worker" or "team player." It shows them
  • The summary says what kind of WORK, not what kind of person
  • Languages are there

Teacher notes

Six things, thirty seconds each. Students circle the summary line and underline the output in three bullets on their own copy.

Slide 5

The seven sections, again

  • Name and contact: town, school email, phone optional. Not your street address
  • Summary: one sentence
  • Skills: six to nine. End with languages
  • Experience: strongest first. School, volunteer, family, paid
  • Activities. Awards. Languages

Teacher notes

Leave this up through the drafting block. It is the slide students will look at while you are in conferences.

Slide 6

The three-part bullet, again

  • The work. The explanation. The output.
  • Verb first. Not "I"
  • A number, a quantity, or a named group of people in every bullet

Teacher notes

Also stays up. The two-minute conference will fix the bullets one at a time; this slide fixes the ones you do not get to.

Slide 7

While you draft, I am calling people up

  • Two minutes each, in order
  • Bring the draft and the mining sheet
  • Everyone gets one, today or tomorrow
  • Nobody waits to keep working

Teacher notes

Say the last line twice. Students will stop writing while they wait if you let them. Start the rotation from yesterday's exit-card pile.

Slide 8

The conference, in four moves

  • Read me your summary line
  • I pick your weakest bullet
  • Three questions: how many? for whom? what came out of it?
  • Say the fixed bullet out loud. Then write it

Teacher notes

Putting the script on a slide makes the conference predictable, which makes it faster and less frightening. Students rehearse it in their heads before they get called.

Slide 9

Partner check. Eight items.

  • All seven sections have something in them
  • One page, no paragraphs
  • A summary line that names a kind of work
  • Five bullets with all three parts
  • Four bullets with a number or a named group
  • Experience includes something unpaid
  • Languages listed
  • Every line is something my partner can point to. I asked about one

Teacher notes

Read all eight aloud once. Set the rule: you mark the checklist, you do not rewrite your partner's resume, and every "no" comes with the missing part named.

Slide 10

Before you file it

  • Make at least one change from the partner check
  • Write this sentence at the bottom:
  • "An employer can now picture ___, which they could not before"
  • Two volunteers read theirs

Teacher notes

Collect the draft, the mining sheet, and the marked checklist as one set. Then: "Tomorrow you find out what happens when somebody asks you about this out loud."

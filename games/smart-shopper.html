Smart Shopper

A price and a quantity, or two options, appear. Tap the unit price or the better buy from three choices. Then a grocery item appears, and you tap the store section it lives in from six.

How to play

  • Unit price = price divided by quantity. Write the dollar sign on every unit price answer. 1 pound (lb) = 16 ounces (oz).
  • Produce (fresh fruits and vegetables); Dairy (milk, cheese, yogurt, eggs, butter); Meat and deli (including pre-cooked chicken); Frozen; Dry goods (pasta, rice, cans, jars, spices, bread); Household (foil, plates, soap, cups).

Round 1

    Done

    The ones to study:

      Worth knowing by heart:

      • Unit price = price divided by quantity; dollar sign on every answer
      • Match the units before you compare: 1 lb = 16 oz
      • Store brand is usually 20 to 40 percent less; bulk wins per ounce only if you can use it and store it
      • Cheaper per unit is the wrong buy when it will spoil first, there is no room to store it, the family will not eat that much, a food rule rules it out, or you cannot afford the bigger total today
      • Most grocery food in New York is not taxed; foil, plates, cups, soap, candy, and soda are

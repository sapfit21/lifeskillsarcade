This is your ledger for the Gilded Age Market. It is the whole game. The teacher is the market: every round, the year and the prices go on the board, you write them in the price table, you work out what you hold, you hear the news, and then you trade on paper. Your ledger is your proof. The Investor Report is written from it, and the leaderboard is built from it, by investor number, never by name.

The prices on the board are simplified and rounded so the arithmetic works. The direction of every move is what really happened.

The rules.

  • You start in 1870 (Round 0) with $1,000 cash and no shares.
  • Whole shares only. Cash may never go below zero.
  • A buy: cash goes down by shares times price. Shares go up.
  • A sell: cash goes up by shares times price. Shares go down. You cannot sell shares you do not hold.
  • The bank: you may move cash into or out of BANK during any round. At the start of Rounds 1 to 8 the bank pays 3 percent of whatever is in it, rounded to the nearest dollar. It never falls. It does not pay at the closing bell.
  • Every trade, and every move into or out of the bank, goes in the trade log, in order, the moment you make it.
  • Every round, fill that round's holdings table: shares times price for each company, plus cash, plus bank, minus any margin loan, equals your total.
  • The margin line opens in Round 6 only. The broker lends you up to the cash you hold, at Round 6 prices. In Round 7 the broker wants it back in cash. If you do not have the cash, you sell shares at Round 7 prices until you do. If your shares are not enough, the loan takes everything and your total is $0.
  • Your trading partner initials one line of every holdings table.

Worked example. Investor 14 has $200 cash in Round 6. She borrows $200, the most allowed, and buys 4 RADIO at 100 for $400. In Round 7, RADIO is 30, so her 4 shares are worth $120. The broker wants $200. She has no cash, so she sells the 4 RADIO for $120 and still owes $80. She sells 2 OIL at 50 for $100, pays the $80, and keeps $20 cash. Her margin line goes to $0.

Lesson 2.16, one day. Slides 1 to 11. Every price is from page 2 of Handout 02.14 - The Gilded Age Market Kit.md. Do not mention the boom or the crash anywhere.

Slide 1

Do now

  • All $1,000 in one company. It drops 50 percent. What is your total now?
  • Now: $1,000 split evenly across five holdings, and only one of them drops 50 percent. What is your total?

Teacher notes

3 minutes. Answers: 500 and 900. Then the eggs.

Slide 2

Same eggs, different morning

  • Twelve eggs in one basket. Drop it.
  • Twelve eggs in three baskets. Drop one.
  • Which investor eats breakfast?

Teacher notes

Image: two drawings: one basket on its side with cracked eggs everywhere; three baskets, one tipped, eight eggs still safe.

Do it with the real (plastic) eggs first. Then the slide.

Slide 3

Diversification

  • Money spread across companies with different risk levels, and the bank.
  • So they do not all fall on the same news.
  • 1873: the railroads fell by almost half. Oil and gas rose, because people still needed light.
  • The bank never cares.

Teacher notes

Image: the price table's Round 1 column drawn large: RAIL with a down arrow, OIL and GAS with small up arrows, BANK with a flat line.

Students copy the definition to the journal's My notes page.

Slide 4

Two pies

  • Pie 1: one slice of 90 percent, a few slivers.
  • Pie 2: six slices, none bigger than a quarter.
  • Pie 1 wins the leaderboard in a boom and sits at the bottom in a panic.
  • Pie 2 never wins big and never loses big.

Teacher notes

Image: two pie charts side by side, labeled with tickers.

Slide 5

Rebalancing

  • Your pie changes shape on its own when prices move.
  • Rebalancing: sell a little of what grew, buy a little of what fell, so the pie looks the way you chose.
  • It is not panic. It is not chasing whatever went up last. It is regular upkeep, like cleaning your room.

Teacher notes

Image: a pie with one slice grown too big, an arrow, and the same pie trimmed back to even.

Slide 6

Three Portfolios and a Shock

  • Three investors, $1,000 each, bought in 1882 at the Round 2 prices.
  • A, the bet: all of it in ELEC and RAIL.
  • B, the vault: most of it in the bank, the rest in GAS.
  • C, the spread: the bank and all four companies.
  • The shock: the Panic of 1893, the prices you lived through two days ago. Then Part 2: hold everything to 1901. Rank them twice.
  • Pairs, calculators. 12 minutes.
Slide 7

Debrief: the Panic of 1893

  • A: $480. Lost $520.
  • B: $988. Lost $12.
  • C: $776. Lost $224.
  • Which one would you want in a panic?

Teacher notes

Image: three bars, 480, 988, 776, with a storm cloud above.

Slide 8

Debrief: held to 1901

  • A: $1,465. Gained $465.
  • B: $1,097. Gained $97.
  • C: $1,294. Gained $294.
  • Which one would you want in a recovery (when prices climb back)?
  • You do not know which is coming. That is the real situation.

Teacher notes

Image: the same three bars, taller, with a sun above.

Slide 9

Round 5, 1911. My own pie.

  • Sketch your pie from your Round 4 holdings on Entry 5. Label the slices.
  • If one slice is bigger than half: rebalance, or hold and say why.
  • Add the bank's 3 percent first. Fill the column: RAIL 65, OIL 60, GAS 32, ELEC 55, STEEL 45, and two new lines, AUTO 20 and RADIO 6.
  • Question from the dispatch: the government broke up the biggest monopoly in the country and its owners got richer. Why?

Teacher notes

Image: a Model T on a dirt road beside a farm, 1911.

10 minutes. Read the Round 5 dispatch after the totals are done. Four minutes of trading. Ask at the desk: what is your biggest slice? Say nothing about the next two rounds.

Slide 10

Entry 5

  • What I did (use the word rebalance or hold). Why. What happened.
  • My biggest slice is ___ percent, and I am comfortable / not comfortable with that because ___.
  • My total at the bell, in dollars.

Teacher notes

4 minutes.

Slide 11

Closure

  • Exit card: diversification in one sentence, in your own words. Then: my biggest slice is ___ percent.
  • Three investors read the sentence.
  • Tomorrow: another round.

Teacher notes

4 minutes. A sentence that only says "buy more stuff" is "needs more." Keep a straight face about tomorrow.

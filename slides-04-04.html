Slide 3

Energy in a home

  • Heating and cooling: about half
  • Heating water: about a fifth
  • Everything else: lights, fridge, laundry, electronics, cooking

Teacher notes

Image: a pie chart with a furnace icon on the biggest slice, a water heater on the next, and a bulb, a fridge, and a TV on the rest.

Rounded from U.S. EIA residential data. Students fill Part 1.

Slide 4

Water in a home

  • Toilets: about a quarter
  • Showers: a fifth
  • Faucets: a fifth
  • Laundry: a sixth
  • Leaks: about a tenth. A tenth of the water bill goes down a drip.

Teacher notes

Image: a pie chart with a toilet, a showerhead, a faucet, a washer, and a drip icon.

Rounded from EPA WaterSense.

Slide 6

Phantom load

  • A device that draws power while "off": chargers, TVs, cable boxes, game consoles, microwaves with clocks
  • About 5 to 10 percent of a home's electricity
  • Watch the meter

Teacher notes

Image: a plug-in electricity meter with a game console plugged in, reading 14 W.

If you have the meter, plug in the biggest phantom you tested. If not, the number on the slide stands.

Slide 7

Two words

  • Conservation: using less of a resource on purpose so it lasts (the state's definition in plain words)
  • Efficiency: getting the same result with less
  • Turning off the light is conservation. The LED is efficiency. You want both.

Teacher notes

CTE SUS 3. d) is "define conservation as it applies to energy." Students write both definitions.

Slide 8

The math, one bulb

  • 60 W x 5 hours x 365 days = 109,500 Wh
  • Divide by 1,000 = 109.5 kWh
  • x $0.25 = $27.38 a year
  • The LED: 9 W. 16,425 Wh = 16.4 kWh = $4.11
  • Savings: $23.27 per bulb. Twenty bulbs: about $465.

Teacher notes

Image: the two bulbs side by side with the wattage circled on each.

Work it on the board with the template. "Watt-hours are pennies, kilowatt-hours are dollars."

Slide 9

The math, a drip and a thermostat

  • One drip per second is about 5 gallons a day
  • 5 x 365 = 1,825 gallons a year, about 30 bathtubs
  • Thermostat: each degree set back for 8 hours saves about 1 percent of the heating bill
  • 7 degrees at night saves about 7 percent. The fix is a sweater.

Teacher notes

Image: a faucet with one drop falling, and a thermostat dial turned to 65.

Pairs do the three practice problems on the handout, Part 2. Check with the pair beside them.

Slide 10

The audit checklist

  • Lights: on in empty rooms? LED?
  • Phantom: chargers, screens, consoles
  • Heat: thermostat, open windows, blocked vents, drafts
  • Water: drips, a running toilet, shower length
  • Appliances: fridge seal, full loads, cold laundry
  • Score each line: OK, Fix (free), Fix ($)

Teacher notes

Walk the twelve lines in Part 3.

Slide 15

Audit the room

  • Take the checklist and a clipboard to your zone

Slide 16

Audit the apartment

  • The Marchetti-Oyelaran apartment, Part 4
  • Two adults, one child, a rental; the landlord pays for heat, the family pays for electricity and hot water
  • Read it room by room; score the same twelve lines
  • Ten minutes

Teacher notes

Watch for the heat lines. The family does not pay for heat, which changes who should fix what.

Slide 17

Twelve fixes

  • Each has a cost and an estimated yearly saving
  • Cross out the two that cost more than $50
  • Rank the other ten by savings, highest first
  • Payback = cost / savings per year

Teacher notes

Image: the twelve-fix table from the handout.

Common mistake is ranking by cost. Say it twice: cheapest first is the filter; savings is the rank.

Slide 18

Who pays?

  • The landlord pays for heat. Who should buy the weatherstripping?
  • The family pays for hot water. Who should buy the low-flow showerhead?
  • The fridge seal is torn. Whose fridge is it?

Teacher notes

Take three answers. This comes back in the Design a Room budget.

Slide 19

Read the bill

  • Electricity used: 650 kWh
  • Supply $0.12 per kWh; delivery $0.13 per kWh; together $0.25
  • Basic service charge $18: the same every month, even at zero
  • Total $180.50
  • Ten LEDs save about $230 a year: next month's bill drops about $19

Teacher notes

Image: the sample bill from the handout with the five numbers boxed.

Slide 20

Exit card

  • One sentence: why the first one is first

Teacher notes

Slide 21

Reset the room

  • Turn off what we turned on to find it
  • Chargers unplugged, windows closed
  • This room is now audited. Keep it that way.

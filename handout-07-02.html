Page 1: The worked example

This resume belongs to a made-up eighth grader named Dev Okonkwo. Dev is not a real person. Every single line on this resume came from something this class actually does, which means you can write one just like it. As you read, circle the summary line and underline the output in three of the bullets.

DEV OKONKWO

Summary: Grade 8 student looking for summer work in a kitchen, a store, or a camp. I can follow a written plan under a time limit, handle money and prices, and work a station with a team of seven.

  • * *

Look at what this resume does that most first drafts do not:

  1. It is one page and there are no paragraphs.
  2. Every bullet has a number in it, or a named audience, or both.
  3. The experience section leads with school and family work, because that is what a 13-year-old has.
  4. It never says "hard worker," "responsible," or "team player." It shows those things instead.
  5. The summary line says what kind of work, not what kind of person.
  6. The languages section is there, and it is the section most students forget.

Partner check

Swap with a partner. Your partner marks these eight items on your sheet. Mark honestly. A "no" with the missing part named helps more than a "yes" that is not true.

Partner's name: ____________________

#CheckYesNo
1All seven sections have something in them. No empty headings.
2It fits on one page and there are no paragraphs.
3There is a summary line and it names a kind of work, not a personality.
4At least five bullets have all three parts (the work, the explanation, the output).
5At least four bullets have a number, a quantity, or a named group of people in them.
6The experience section includes something that was not a paid job.
7Languages are listed.
8Every line is something my partner can point to. I asked about one and they showed me.

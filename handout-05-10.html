Part 3: Threading, step by step

Every machine is a little different. The manual at your machine is the authority, not the teacher.

Threading the top

Step 1. Put the spool on the spool pin.
Picture: a thread spool pushed down onto a short upright pin on top of the machine.

Step 2. Pull the thread through the first guide.
Picture: thread running from the spool to the right, through a small metal hook near the top.

Step 3. Bring the thread down and around the tension dial.
Picture: thread running down the left face of the machine, looping around a numbered dial, and back up.

Step 4. Bring the thread up and through the take-up lever, back to front.
Picture: thread going up into a slotted metal arm and out through the hole at its end.

Step 5. Bring the thread down through the lower guides.
Picture: thread running down past two small hooks toward the needle.

Step 6. Thread the needle, front to back.
Picture: thread going through the needle's eye from the front side of the machine toward the back, with about four inches hanging out.

The bobbin

Step 7. Take a wound bobbin, or wind one.
Picture: a small spool of thread held in the fingers.

Step 8. Open the plate and drop the bobbin in the case the way the arrow shows.
Picture: a sliding plate open in the machine bed, a bobbin sitting in a round case, and a small printed arrow showing which way the thread unwinds.

Step 9. Pull the bobbin thread through the slot.
Picture: bobbin thread pulled sideways into a narrow groove in the case.

Step 10. Close the plate.
Picture: the sliding plate closed flat over the bobbin area.

Step 11. Hold the top thread and turn the hand wheel one full turn toward you.
Picture: one hand holding the top thread lightly, the other turning the big wheel toward the front, and a small loop of bobbin thread appearing through the hole in the plate.

Step 12. Pull the loop up and lay both threads back under the presser foot.
Picture: two threads, top and bobbin, pulled back behind the needle and lying under and behind the foot.

The threading license

Thread the top and load the bobbin at your own machine while your buddy checks every step against this guide. When every step matches, your buddy initials. Then the teacher checks the tension and the bobbin drop and initials. Two initials is a license: from then on you sit at a machine you threaded yourself.

Buddy check, every step matched: ______ (buddy initials) Teacher check: ______ (teacher initials)

Part 4: Paper practice record

No thread in the machine. The needle punches holes in the paper, and the holes show exactly how straight your line was. Hold the finished sheet up to the light.

Part 5: The blanket stitch (no-machine path)

What it is for: it finishes a raw edge so it cannot fray, and it decorates at the same time. It is the stitch for felt edges, blanket edges, and appliqué. A machine cannot do it the way a hand can.

Step 1. Knot your thread and come up a quarter inch in from the edge.
Picture: a needle coming up through felt a quarter inch from the edge, with the knot underneath.

Step 2. Bring the needle around and over the edge.
Picture: an arrow looping the thread over the top of the edge from back to front.

Step 3. Go back in a quarter inch along, and a quarter inch in from the edge.
Do not pull it tight yet.
Picture: the needle entering the felt a quarter inch further along the edge, with thread still loose.

Step 4. Before you pull, pass the needle through the loop of thread lying along the edge.
This is the step that makes it a blanket stitch instead of a whipstitch.
Picture: the needle tip passing under a loop of thread that runs along the very edge of the felt.

Step 5. Pull snug.
The thread now lies along the edge with a little bar going over it, like a capital L on its side.
Picture: a finished blanket stitch: a line of thread running along the edge with evenly spaced bars standing up from it into the fabric.

Step 6. Repeat, keeping the spacing even.
Picture: six or seven blanket stitches marching along an edge, all the same width apart.

Step 7. At a corner, take two or three stitches into the same corner hole and fan them.
Picture: three stitches radiating out from one hole at a corner of the felt, like a small fan.

Step 8. Finish off.
Three tiny stitches in one place, then a knot, then trim.
Picture: snips cutting a thread tail close to a finished blanket-stitched edge.

Teacher key

Part 1 and Part 2 have many right answers. Score them complete, partial, or missing. The four jobs must all be filled in with a matching example; the example that students copy from the slide instead of writing their own is the one to push back on.

Part 2, the expected answers:

Part 3, what counts as a Meets answer. The outfit does not have to match any single "right" answer. It has to be defensible, include footwear, and the reason sentence has to name all three: weather, activity, setting. The most common failures and the correct pushes:

CardThe usual mistakeThe push
Job interviewNo shoes named, or sneakers with no reasoning"What do the shoes say in this setting?"
Construction siteSneakers, shorts, no head protection"What is overhead? What could be dropped on a foot?" A hard hat, long pants, and closed sturdy shoes are the point
WeddingTurns into a fashion conversation"What does the family expect of a guest?" Then add the grass and the 84 degrees, which rules out some footwear
Soccer gameCotton shirt for a wet gameThis previews Lesson 5.3: a wicking fabric beats cotton here, and that is the fiber lesson coming
Cold walkOne thick coat, no layers, no rain plan"Three thin layers or one thick one? Which one lets you take something off when you get inside?"

Part 4, the sort. Most rules belong in more than one box, and saying so is the point.

RuleExpected boxes
Restaurant kitchenSafety (non-slip shoes, jewelry in machinery), Sanitation (hair, jewelry in food), Image (the customer sees the staff)
HospitalSafety, Sanitation (nails and rings carry bacteria into a sterile room), Team identity (scrubs identify the role)
This schoolAnswers vary by code. Most school codes are Image and Team identity, with a Safety line for shop and lab classes. Do not lead students to a verdict on this school's code; the reasoning is the graded part

The "what it costs" sentence earns credit for naming money, comfort, time, or self-expression. A blank or "nothing" gets one question at the desk: "What if the shoes cost sixty dollars and you only need them for work?"

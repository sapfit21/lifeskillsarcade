Lesson 5.13, one day. Every dollar figure on this sheet is written for class and to be refreshed before teaching.

The formula: cost per wear = price divided by the number of times worn. The answer is in dollars per wear.

Part 1. The three worked problems

Show the division. Write the unit on every answer.

(a) The jeans. A pair of jeans costs $60. You wear them 200 times before they wear out.

$60 ÷ 200 wears = $__________ per wear

(b) The shirt. A shirt costs $15. You wore it 5 times and then stopped wearing it.

$15 ÷ 5 wears = $__________ per wear

(c) The coat. A winter coat costs $90. You wear it for 3 winters.

First you have to estimate. How many times do you wear a winter coat in one winter? ________ times

So 3 winters is 3 × ________ = ________ wears

$90 ÷ ________ wears = $__________ per wear

Which of the three cost the most per wear? ______________________

Which one had the lowest price? ______________________

Part 2. What makes a garment last

Fill in what a good one looks like and what a fail looks like.

FactorWhat to look forA good oneA fail
1. FiberRead the label. What is it made of, and in what percentages?
2. Seam typeLook inside. One row of stitching, two rows, or a raw edge wrapped inside?
3. Stitches per inchPut the ruler on a seam and count one inch.______ per inch is good______ per inch is low
4. Reinforcement at stress pointsLook where the body pulls: pocket corners, belt loops, underarms, knees, where a strap joins.
5. Buttons and closuresPull the button gently. Run the zipper.

Seam words to know

  • Single-needle seam: one row of stitching. It is the first seam to open.
  • Double-needle seam: two parallel rows. It holds much better.
  • Flat felled seam: the raw edges are folded and wrapped inside, then stitched through. The strongest of the three. Look at the outside side seam of a pair of jeans.
  • Serged edge: thread looped over the raw edge to stop it fraying. That is a finish, not a seam. A serged edge alone does not hold two pieces together.
  • Bar tack: a short, dense line of stitches at a stress point, like the corner of a pocket.
  • Stress point: anywhere the body pulls on the garment.

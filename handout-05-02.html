Teacher key

Part 1, the four channels.

ChannelWhat it isExample
WordsThe words you chooseSaying "I can help you with that"
VoiceTone, volume, speed, pausesThe same sentence said warmly or flatly
BodyFace, hands, posture, distance, eye contactArms crossed, a shrug, turning away
AppearanceClothing, grooming, what you carryA suit, a uniform, a team shirt

Clothing lives in channel 4, appearance.

A uniform says: what job or role the person has, what group or team they belong to, that they are on duty, who to ask for help. Personal choice is taken off the table on purpose.

A costume says: this is not me today, it is temporary, and it is on purpose.

What has to be true for a costume to work: the person wearing it and the people looking at it both have to know it is a costume.

Part 2, the same shirt. Answers vary; a Meets answer names something different in each row and the "what changed" sentence names the setting or the audience, not the shirt. Common correct answers: interview, "I am serious and I prepared," read by a manager; cookout, "I am comfortable and I am family," read by relatives; hallway, "this is my normal," read by classmates. A student who writes the same thing in all three rows has missed the point; ask "who is reading it in row 1 versus row 3?"

Part 3. 3a: any accurate example. Items named on the slide are a hijab, a sari, a kilt, a kente cloth, a guayabera, a kufi, and a huipil. Accept family or community examples the student knows. 3b: because an item that belongs to a living culture is somebody's real clothing, not a temporary pretend, and treating it as a costume says their real life is a joke. 3c and 3d: answers vary; the point of 3d is that every generation got criticized and every generation thought it was unfair.

Part 4, the agreement. There is no key, because the class writes it. Lines that usually come up and are worth steering toward:

Okay to say: a compliment about the item, not about the body; "where is that from"; "I like your jacket"; nothing at all; a question asked once, privately, and dropped if the answer is short.

Not okay to say: pricing somebody's clothes out loud, real or guessed; commenting on the same person twice; "you always wear that"; a joke about a religious or cultural item; a comment about whether clothing fits somebody's body; saying it online about a photo.

Score Objective 3 from who nominated a line and gave a reason, noted on the clipboard during the discussion. The signed chart paper is the class artifact and stays posted through Lesson 5.18.

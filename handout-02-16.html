Three investors each had $1,000 in 1882 and bought at the Round 2 prices: RAIL 45, OIL 30, GAS 26, ELEC 25. Whole shares only, so a few dollars stayed in cash. Then the Panic of 1893 hit, the Round 3 prices you lived through two days ago. Then, in Part 2, they held everything to 1901, the Round 4 prices. Your job: find out what happened to each investor both times.

Risk levels for this sheet (from the company cards): ELEC high at first; RAIL medium, in a panic high; OIL medium; GAS low; BANK none.

The bank rule. The bank pays 3 percent of the balance at the start of every round, rounded to the nearest dollar. From 1882 to 1893 it paid once. From 1893 to 1901 it paid once more.

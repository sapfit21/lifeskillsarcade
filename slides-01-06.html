Lesson 1.6, Unit 1, Topic 1.2. Two days. Day 1 is slides 1 to 11; Day 2 is slides 12 to 22.

Day 1

Slide 1

Do now

  • A recipe says 1 c flour.
  • Your friend scoops the cup into the bag and packs it down.
  • Is that 1 cup? Yes or no, and why. Two minutes.
Slide 2

The scale does not lie

  • One cup of flour, packed: about 150 grams.
  • One cup of flour, spooned and leveled: about 120 grams.
  • That is a quarter cup extra. That is why the cookies were bricks.

Teacher notes

Image: two measuring cups on a scale, side by side, with the two readings.

Do this live. Pack one, spoon the other, weigh both. Let them see the number.

Slide 3

Three kinds, three tools

  • Dry (flour, sugar, cocoa): dry measuring cups.
  • Liquid (water, milk, oil): liquid measuring cup.
  • Small amounts (salt, baking powder, vanilla): measuring spoons.

Teacher notes

Image: the three tools with the three ingredient groups.

Slide 4

Dry technique

  • Spoon it into the cup. Do not scoop, do not pack, do not tap.
  • Fill over the top.
  • Level off with a straight edge (the back of a butter knife).
  • Exception: brown sugar is packed. The recipe will say so.

Teacher notes

Image: a hand leveling a cup of flour with a butter knife, flour falling into a tray.

Slide 5

Liquid technique

  • Set the glass cup on the counter. Do not hold it in the air.
  • Pour to the line.
  • Bend down. Read at eye level. The bottom of the curve (the meniscus) sits on the line.
  • From above, you will read it high every time.

Teacher notes

Image: a side view of a liquid measuring cup at eye level with the meniscus on the 1 cup line.

Slide 6

Small amounts

  • Dry: fill the spoon, level it.
  • Liquid: fill to the brim.
  • Do it over a bowl, not over the mix. A spill goes in the bowl, not the recipe.
  • 1/4 tsp is real. It matters. Baking powder proves it.

Teacher notes

Image: measuring spoons fanned out with the sizes labeled.

Slide 7

Abbreviations

  • tsp = teaspoon
  • Tbsp = tablespoon (the big T is the big spoon)
  • c = cup
  • oz = ounce (fl oz for liquids)
  • lb = pound

Teacher notes

Image: each abbreviation next to a picture of the tool.

Slide 8

Equivalents (say them twice)

  • 3 tsp = 1 Tbsp
  • 16 Tbsp = 1 c
  • 4 Tbsp = 1/4 c
  • 8 fl oz = 1 c (liquid)
  • 2 c = 1 pint; 16 oz = 1 lb (by weight)

Teacher notes

Chant them. Then cover the right side and chant again.

Slide 9

Practice stations

  • A flour, B sugar, C water, D oil, E salt.
  • Two amounts at each station. Right tool, right technique.
  • If your station has a scale, weigh the first amount and write the grams.
  • Return the ingredient to the container. Spills go on the tray.
  • Three minutes per station.
Slide 10

Equivalents practice

  • Six problems on the handout, Part 3.
  • Alone first. Then compare with a partner.
  • Problem 6 is a real one: rice for 4 people becomes rice for 8.
  • Grade 8: problems 7 and 8.
Slide 11

Closure

  • Exit card: 3 equivalents from memory, 2 techniques you will use tomorrow, 1 thing that still confuses you.
  • Tomorrow: nobody talks. Six measurements. One scale. Points.
  • * *

Day 2

Slide 12

Do now

  • Write one way to tell a teammate "stop, that is too much" without using your voice.
  • Two minutes. Then two people show theirs.
Slide 13

The Silent Measurement Challenge

  • Six measurements on your team card.
  • One scale. The scale is the judge.
  • Points for accuracy. 18 possible.
  • Nobody talks.

Teacher notes

Image: a tray with six numbered cups and a scale beside it.

Slide 14

The rules

  • No talking, no notes, no mouthing words.
  • Pointing, gestures, fingers, and showing the card are allowed.
  • A different person measures each cup. Everyone else watches and signals.
  • Once a cup is on the tray, it is done. Do not touch it again.
  • Right tool for each kind. Carry the tray to the weigh-in together.

Teacher notes

Model one silent correction: hold up a flat hand, point at the line on the cup.

Slide 15

Points

  • 3: in the tight band.
  • 2: in the wide band.
  • 1: right ingredient, wrong amount.
  • 0: wrong ingredient or empty cup.
  • Team totals go on the board.
Slide 16

Go

  • 12 minutes.
  • Time calls at 6 and 10.
  • I say nothing else.

Teacher notes

Image: a large timer.

Slide 17

Weigh-in

  • Tare the scale on an empty cup.
  • Each cup, one at a time. I read the grams out loud.
  • Record your grams and points on the handout, Part 4.
  • Mark the cup that missed.

Teacher notes

Image: the scoreboard grid with four team rows.

Slide 18

Why measuring matters, baking chemistry

  • Flour is the structure.
  • Sugar is the sweetness and the browning.
  • Baking powder is the lift.
  • Fat (oil, butter) is the tenderness.
  • Too much of one throws off the others. A cake is a ratio.

Teacher notes

Image: a simple bar for each ingredient labeled with its job.

Slide 19

Reading the miss

  • Flour heavy: packed or scooped.
  • Flour light: liquid cup read from above, or tapped and settled.
  • Sugar off: not leveled.
  • Water off: read from above (heavy) or spilled on the way (light).
  • Salt heavy: a heaped spoon.
  • Which one was your team's miss?
Slide 20

How did you talk?

  • You could not use words. What did you use?
  • The signal that worked.
  • The moment you wished you could talk.
  • This has a name: nonverbal communication. Cooks use it in every loud kitchen.

Teacher notes

One team at a time. Write the signals on the board. Say out loud that the students who are quiet in English are often the best at this game.

Slide 21

Grade 8 stretch

  • Percent error for each cup: (your grams minus target) divided by target, times 100.
  • Which cup had the biggest percent error? Was it the biggest gram error?
Slide 22

Closure

  • Exit card, one sentence: "Next time I measure ___ I will ___ because the scale showed ___."
  • Two students read theirs.
  • Tomorrow: reading the recipe you will cook.

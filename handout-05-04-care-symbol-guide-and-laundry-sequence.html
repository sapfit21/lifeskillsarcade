Teacher key

Part 1, the five families.

FamilyShapeDotsBarsX over it
WashingWashtubTemperature: one dot coolest, more dots hotterBe gentler: one bar permanent press, two bars delicateDo not wash in water
BleachingTriangleNoneNoneDo not bleach. An empty triangle means any bleach; diagonal lines inside mean non-chlorine bleach only
DryingSquareHeat level in the tumble dryerBe gentler in the dryerDo not tumble dry
IroningIron shapeHeat level: one dot low, two medium, three highNoneDo not iron
Professional careCircleNoneBe gentler in the cleaning processDo not dry clean

Extra marks: a hand inside the washtub means hand wash only. A circle inside the square means tumble dry. A line inside the square means line dry (hang it). A P or an F inside the circle tells the dry cleaner which solvent to use. An X through the steam lines means no steam while ironing.

Why the label is there: in the United States a permanent care label is required in most garments by the Federal Trade Commission's Care Labeling Rule, so the manufacturer has to tell the buyer how to clean the item. That is a consumer protection, which is why CTE FCL 4. h) is cited on this lesson.

Part 2. Answers depend on the actual basket. Score complete, partial, or missing: complete means every garment placed with the symbol that decided it named. The whites-and-colors split matters because dye bleeds in water, especially in warm water and especially the first few washes, so a red shirt in a load of whites turns the load pink; also because whites can take bleach and colors cannot.

Part 3.

What happenedFiber reasonThe symbolReplacement, about
Hot water and hot dryer on woolHeat and agitation make the scales on the wool fiber lock together; the sweater felts, shrinks, and stiffens, permanentlyA hand in the washtub (hand wash only) or an X over the tumble dry square$42
Hot dryer on spandex leggingsHeat breaks down the elastic fiber so it stops springing back; the knees stay baggyA square with a line inside (line dry) or a low or no heat dryer symbol$14
Chlorine bleach on a colored shirtBleach strips the dye, in patches, and there is no way to put it backA triangle with an X, or a triangle with diagonal lines (non-chlorine only)$12
Hot iron on polyesterPolyester melts instead of burning, so the plate leaves a shine or a holeAn iron with one dot (low heat) or an iron with an X$20

Total, about $88 from one bad load. [Sal: use your own prices; the total is the point, not the numbers.]

Part 4, the sequence and the reasons.

StepWhat you doWhy
SortBy color and by care symbol; check pocketsDye bleeds; a wool and a towel do not want the same water; a pen in a pocket ruins a load
Treat a stainPretreat before it goes inA wash sets a stain and a dryer bakes it in permanently
LoadLoose, not packedClothes need room to move or they do not get clean
DetergentThe amount on the cap for the load sizeExtra detergent stays in the fabric, holds dirt, and stiffens the cloth
WashThe temperature and cycle the tag saysThe tag is the manufacturer telling you what the fiber can survive
Dry or hangThe square symbol decides; hang anything with spandex, wool, or a printHeat is what ruins most garments
Fold or hang upRight awayA wrinkle set in a hot pile is an ironing job you did not have to do

Daily: hang it up or put it in the hamper, check for a stain, air out what you wore. Weekly: the wash, and check what needs a repair. Seasonal: store it clean, folded or hung, in a closed container; check for missing buttons before you put it away.

Storing dirty: sweat, food, and body oils attract insects such as clothes moths and carpet beetles, and stains set permanently over months.

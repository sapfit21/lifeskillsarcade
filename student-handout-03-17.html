Part 1: The seven rules

#RuleWhyPicture
1Never leave a young child alone. Not for a minute, not to answer the door. Take them with you.A minute is enough.an eye on a child
2Safe sleep: a baby sleeps on their back, in a crib or bassinet, on a firm flat mattress, with nothing else in it. No blanket, no pillow, no stuffed animal, no bumper.Anything soft can block breathing. Back is the only safe position.an empty crib, baby on back
3Never shake a baby. Ever. If a baby will not stop crying: put them down safely on their back in the empty crib, step out for two minutes, breathe, check them, then call the parent.A baby's neck and brain cannot take it. The damage is permanent.a crossed-out shaking motion
4Small objects and choking foods stay out of reach. Anything that fits through a toilet paper tube can choke a child under 3. A child under 4 eats sitting down with an adult watching. No whole grapes, hot dogs, hard candy, nuts, popcorn, or chunks of raw carrot or apple.The airway is the size of a straw.a tube with a coin next to it
5Water and stairs: never look away. A child can drown in two inches of water. The bath is never left, not for a towel. Stairs get a gate or a hand.Two inches. Two seconds.a bathtub, a gate
6In an emergency, call 911 first for anything you cannot handle. Know the address. Know the parent's number and a backup. Then call the parent.The dispatcher needs the address first.a phone with 911
7Know when to say no to a job. Short jobs, a child old enough to talk, a parent you know and can reach, a house you have seen, daylight or early evening, no infants overnight.Ready is a real thing.a thumbs up and a thumbs down

Part 2: The safety walk

You are babysitting a 2 year old named Eli at his home for the first time. Walk through it on paper. Circle every hazard for a toddler. Write the fix in the table. There are at least twelve.

The living room. A couch with a throw blanket. A coffee table with a glass bowl of hard candy, a TV remote, and a set of keys. A floor lamp with the cord running across the walkway to the outlet. A bookshelf with a heavy vase on the top shelf and no wall strap. Two uncovered outlets behind the couch. A basket of Eli's toys, including a set of large blocks and one small bouncy ball.

The kitchen. A stove with a pot on the front burner, handle turned out. A dishwasher, open, with knives pointing up in the basket. Under the sink, a bottle of spray cleaner and a box of dishwasher pods, the cabinet with no latch. A bowl of grapes on the counter at the edge. A plastic bag from the store on the floor. A dog's water bowl by the back door.

The bathroom. A bathtub with two inches of water still in it from the morning. A bottle of children's medicine on the counter, cap on. A hair dryer plugged in next to the sink. The toilet lid up. A bath mat.

The bedroom. A crib with a soft blanket, a pillow, and two stuffed animals in it. A window with a blind cord hanging down to crib height. A dresser with a lamp on top and drawers that slide out easily. A nightlight.

The back door and yard. A back door that does not latch. Three steps down to the yard with no railing. A bucket half full of rainwater by the fence. A small plastic slide. A gate to the street, open.

Part 4: Should you take this job?

For each, write yes, yes with conditions, or no, and the rule that decides it.

JobYes / conditions / noThe rule that decides it

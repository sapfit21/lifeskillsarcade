The four constraints

Every project. No exceptions.

  1. It must start from a used textile. Not new fabric off a roll.
  2. It must be finished in two build days (Lesson 5.17, two periods of 40 minutes).
  3. It must use at least two of the taught joins (hand stitches or a machine seam): running stitch, backstitch, whipstitch, sewing on a button.
  4. It must be something somebody would actually use, and you have to name who.

Family and Consumer Sciences, Unit 5: the Upcycle Challenge

Dear family,

For the next six class days my students are turning a used piece of cloth into something useful, by hand, with a needle and thread. No sewing machine is needed; every path can be built by hand. We cover needle and pin safety every day, we count every needle and pin out at the start of the period and back in at the end, and nobody leaves the room until the counts match.

Your student does not need to bring anything from home. Our classroom has a bin of donated and scrap fabric, and every student can take what they need from it. That is the normal way to do this project and it earns full credit.

If your student would rather use something of their own, it has to be something you are willing to have cut up and sewn, because it will not go back to being what it was. Please sign below only if that is the case. Nothing valuable, nothing borrowed, and nothing that belongs to somebody else.

If you have clean, used textiles you would like to donate to the class bin (t-shirts, towels, pillowcases, jeans, sweaters with holes, sheets, fabric scraps), we will take them and everyone will use them. Donating is not required and is not tracked.

Thank you,

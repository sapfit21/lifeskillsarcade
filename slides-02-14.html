Lesson 2.14, two days. Day 1: slides 1 to 10. Day 2: slides 11 to 20. Check the currency and the starting purse against the live site before class.

Slide 1

Do now (Day 1)

  • You have $100 you will not need for a whole year.
  • Where do you put it: a jar at home, a savings account at a bank, or your cousin's new sneaker business?
  • One sentence: why.

Teacher notes

Hands for each. Count the business votes; that is the pre-assessment.

Slide 2

Three real choices

  • The jar: safest. Pays nothing.
  • The bank: almost as safe. Pays a little.
  • The business: could double. Could lose it all.
  • Adults make this choice every month. For ten days, you make it seven times over.

Teacher notes

Image: three drawings side by side: a glass jar with bills, a bank building, a sneaker with a price tag.

Slide 3

Saving

  • Saving: money set aside where it is safe and you can get it back.
  • A savings account pays a small amount every year (about 4 percent right now at a good bank).
  • It never drops. It never gets exciting.

Teacher notes

Image: a piggy bank with a small coin dropping in.

Slide 4

Investing

  • Investing: buying something you expect to be worth more later.
  • No promise. It can go up. It can go down.
  • Stocks, bonds, land, a business.

Teacher notes

Image: a small plant growing out of a coin, with a storm cloud in the corner.

Slide 5

The risk ladder

  • Bottom rung: a jar. Then a savings account. Then a government bond. Then a company bond.
  • Middle: real estate. A retirement fund.
  • Top: a big company's stock. A brand new company's stock.
  • The rule: higher up the ladder, more it can grow, more it can fall.

Teacher notes

Image: a ladder with an icon on each rung: jar, bank, flag, handshake, house, tree, factory, rocket.

Students copy the ladder to the journal cover. Say the rule twice.

Slide 6

Two check questions

  • A savings account pays 4 percent a year and never drops. Where on the ladder?
  • A new company's stock doubled last year and lost 80 percent this year. Where on the ladder?
  • Which one would you want for money you need next month?

Teacher notes

Thumbs, then cold call.

Slide 7

The seven ventures of the Golden Land

  • Sparkstone Mines (SPRK). Cloudberry Expeditions (CLDB). The Iron Vault (IRON).
  • Crown Seal Notes (SEAL). Willowbrook Grove (WLLW). The Honeypot Coffer (HNYP). Meadowgold Acres (MDOW).
  • Your job with your partner: read the seven cards, line them up safest to riskiest, write your risk guess on the journal cover.
  • Nobody learns what they really are until Day 9.

Teacher notes

Image: the seven picture cues from the cards in a row: lightning over a mine, a balloon, an iron door, a sealed scroll, a young tree, a honey jar, a farmhouse.

8 minutes. Circulate. Debrief with the class order on the board.

Slide 8

How to log in

  • Go to ClassroomStreet.com.
  • Choose your class period.
  • Type your merchant number and your PIN from the card.
  • No names. No emails. The card is the only thing you type.
  • Lost card: come to me, not to a friend.

Teacher notes

Image: a drawn login screen with three boxes labeled Period, Merchant Number, PIN.

Slide 9

The trading floor

  • The ticker: seven ventures and today's prices.
  • Buy (the site says Acquire). Sell (the site says Divest).
  • Your cash. Your total fortune. Your starting purse: AQ25,000.
  • Rank titles: Grand Merchant, Master Trader, Silver Purse, Rising Star, Keen Merchant, Journeyman, Apprentice.

Teacher notes

Image: a drawn dashboard: a ticker strip across the top, a list of seven ventures with prices, two buttons, a cash line, a total fortune line.

Model one buy on the projector: 50 units of SEAL. Show the cash go down and the value appear.

Slide 10

Today's two rules, then trade

  • Buy at least three different ventures.
  • Keep at least AQ1,000 in cash.
  • Write your tickers and units on the journal cover as you go.
  • Halfway through, the window changes and prices move. Watch your total fortune.
  • Closure: Entry 1 (what I bought, why, my total fortune) and the 3-2-1 card.

Teacher notes

14 minutes. Advance to window 2 at about the seven-minute mark. Do not tell anyone what to buy.

Slide 11

Do now (Day 2)

  • Log in. Open your portfolio.
  • Write your total fortune at the top of Entry 2.
  • Above or below AQ25,000? By how much?

Teacher notes

3 minutes.

Slide 12

Reading your portfolio screen

  • Cash: what you have not spent.
  • Venture, units held, price now.
  • Value = units times price now.
  • Total fortune = cash + all your values.
  • Gain or loss = total fortune minus 25,000.

Teacher notes

Image: a drawn portfolio screen with each number circled and labeled.

Use the teacher account. Point at every number.

Slide 13

The ledger

  • Every buy and sell, in order: window, ticker, units, price.
  • The ledger is proof. If you think the site is wrong, the ledger settles it.
  • Check one trade: units times price should equal the cash that left.
  • Write the check on Entry 2.

Teacher notes

Image: a drawn ledger table with four rows.

Slide 14

Have you lost money?

  • Your total fortune says 24,600. You started with 25,000.
  • Did you lose 400? Or did the price of something you hold drop?
  • You have not lost anything until you sell.
  • Say it with me.

Teacher notes

This line comes back on Day 6. Plant it now.

Slide 15

Look at your pie

  • Hands: more than half your money in one venture?
  • Hands: money in all seven?
  • Sketch your pie on Entry 2. Label the slices with tickers.

Teacher notes

Image: two pies: one with a 90 percent slice, one with six even slices.

No lecture. "On Day 5 we find out which pie survives a bad week."

Slide 16

Trading window

  • Windows 3 to 6 open now, one at a time.
  • Read every dispatch. Who does it affect?
  • Nobody has to trade. Holding is a decision too.
  • Note every trade on Entry 2 as it happens.

Teacher notes

10 minutes. One click every two and a half minutes. Read the headline aloud.

Slide 17

The Investor Journal: three prompts

  • What I did (bought, sold, or held; which venture; how many units).
  • Why I did it (a dispatch, a risk level, my pie, a feeling you noticed).
  • What happened (did the price move, did my fortune move).
  • Plus the number: my total fortune at the bell.

Teacher notes

Image: a journal page with three boxes and a number line at the bottom.

Slide 18

A model entry

  • "Today I bought 40 units of Meadowgold at 72.80 because the dispatch said planting started and it is medium risk."
  • "The price went to 73.60 by the last window, so my value went up 32."
  • "My total fortune is AQ25,410."
  • Three sentences and the number. That is a complete entry.

Teacher notes

Model it live on the projector, then 10 minutes for Entry 2. Students finish anything missing on Entry 1.

Slide 19

Sentence starters

  • Today I bought ___ because ___. / Hoy compré ___ porque ___.
  • I did not trade today because ___. / Hoy no hice ninguna operación porque ___.
  • My total fortune is ___. / Toplam servetim ___. / Minha fortuna total é ___. / Mi fortuna total es ___.
Slide 20

Closure (Day 2)

  • Two merchants read Entry 2 aloud.
  • Exit card: cash, units, price, value, total fortune. Put all five in one sentence about your own portfolio.
  • Tomorrow: why prices move.

Teacher notes

4 minutes. Sort the cards: all five words used correctly is "got it."

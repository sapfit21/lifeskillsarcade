About the teacher

About the teacher

Salvatore Prevosto, Family and Consumer Sciences.

Who I am

I am Salvatore Prevosto, and I teach Family and Consumer Sciences to middle school students. I came to teaching from business, and I did not come quietly. I got into this because I have seen what one good teacher can do for a kid, and I wanted to be that person for somebody. Most of what I teach is what I wish somebody had taught me at twelve: how to cook a real meal, how to read a paycheck, how to fix a seam, how to plan a room, how to talk about yourself in an interview without shrinking.

Certification and degrees

I am finishing my New York State certification in Family and Consumer Sciences through the Post-Baccalaureate Advanced Certificate program at Queens College. Before that I earned a BS in Corporate Innovation and Entrepreneurship and an MBA, both at Penn State. The business degrees are not a side note. They are the reason the money and career units in this course go as deep as they do.

Before teaching

I spent about ten years around money and people before I stood in front of a class. I worked in business development and then as a business intelligence analyst at a software company, so I read numbers for a living. I sold life insurance and hold FINRA licenses, which taught me how ordinary families actually save, borrow, and invest, and how often nobody explained any of it to them until it was late. I coached fitness classes, where I learned to run a room of thirty people who all showed up with a different reason for being there. And for several summers I managed a hotel and its bar, running food and beverage, point of sale, inventory, and staff training, which is where I learned what a food cost is and why a kitchen runs on a clock.

I teach students to read a Nutrition Facts label and a paycheck stub the same way I learned to read a profit and loss statement: what is the real number, and what is somebody hoping you skip.

What I built

I built ClassroomStreet.com, an investing simulation that more than 150 of my students have traded on. It is its own project, separate from this curriculum. Unit 2 names it as one optional tool for the investing lessons, and the written default there is a paper and spreadsheet budget, so no lesson depends on it.

Why this curriculum exists

I wrote Life Skills Arcade because I wanted to walk into any middle school with a full year of FACS already planned, standards cited line by line, every lab with a no-kitchen version, and every handout ready in the languages my students speak. It is one course for grades 6 to 8, 180 days, eight units, and it is built to be taught, not admired. I expect to revise it every year with the students in front of me.

A few things I believe run through every unit. Kids do not learn from people they do not trust, so the routines are the same every day, the roles are posted, and the rubric goes out before the work starts. Safety comes before any lab, with no exceptions. The way you talk about yourself is a skill, not a personality trait, which is why the year ends with a resume, an interview, and a pitch. And every standard I list is a standard I actually teach and actually assess.

Languages

I speak conversational French. I do not speak Turkish, Portuguese, or Spanish, so the vocabulary tables on this site were built through a translation workflow and are marked for a native speaker to check before they go to students.

Needle sizes run backwards. A size 5 is a bigger, thicker needle than a size 9. Fabric shears cut fabric only, because paper dulls a blade in one cut, and a dull blade makes you push, and pushing is how people get hurt.

Part 3, the six needle rules, in the wording used all unit.

  1. A needle or a pin lives in the pin cushion or the kit. Never on the table, never in your mouth, never in a pocket, never in the chair.
  2. Count out, count in. No station is dismissed until the numbers match.
  3. Snips and shears travel closed and pointing down, and they are handed over handle first.
  4. Fabric shears cut fabric only.
  5. One person operates the iron, hands away from the plate, cord behind and never across a walkway.
  6. Your free hand stays behind the needle, never in front of it. If you get stuck, stop, tell the teacher, wash it, and it gets written up.

The count-out and count-in routine, six steps.

  1. The Station Manager opens the kit and counts out loud, touching each one: needles, then pins.
  2. The Station Manager writes both numbers in today's row on the station count sheet.
  3. The station works. Any needle not in a hand is in the cushion.
  4. At the five-minute warning, everything stops and every needle and pin goes back in the cushion.
  5. The Station Manager counts again, out loud, and writes the second numbers.
  6. The numbers match and the teacher initials the row, or the numbers do not match and nobody leaves that station until it is found.

The machine close-down routine, on machine days, in the wording used all unit (canonical here and in Handout 05.10; change it here and there together, nowhere else).

  1. Presser foot down.
  2. Power off.
  3. Chair in.

A broken needle is reported and handed to the teacher, both pieces, flat on an open palm. It is never thrown out and never left on the floor.
